## 1. Pre-generated content (made with AI tools during development)

**Answer: Yes.**

Draft text for the free-text description (plain, specific, for players and reviewers):

> Early concept art for the player's first mech chassis was made with an AI image generator (Midjourney). A Claude-based coding assistant drove the Midjourney web interface through computer use, from prompts written by the development team and recorded in full, in 15 image generations (a 4-image grid each) of a budget of 16. A human-reviewed selection of nine of those images was kept as reference sheets: silhouettes, a turnaround, plate and joint studies, and a mood frame. None of these images is in the game or on the store page.
>
> The mech's 3D model was then built from the team's written design specification and those reference sheets by a script in Blender (a deterministic, re-runnable script with exact dimensions; no AI-generated mesh, texture or image was imported), and brought into Unity by an import script. The mech's materials are flat colours taken from the game's palette file. In other words the shipped mech is script-built geometry that was informed by AI concept art; it contains no AI-generated pixels.
>
> The map of the San Francisco Bay Area is built from public geographic data (OpenStreetMap and US public-domain sources), credited in the game. Other game code, shaders and tools are written by the team with an AI coding assistant; this is development tooling and not content players receive.

What to enter in the form: pre-generated = **Yes**; tool = Midjourney (concept art), Claude (coding assistant, tooling only); the paragraph above.

Why we disclose concept art that never ships: Valve's section concerns content that ships, but the concept art is a direct reference for a shipped model. A one-sentence disclosure is cheaper than a mismatch finding (Q-NEW-120e).
