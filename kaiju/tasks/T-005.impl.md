## Decisions (each was needed to continue; none waited for a founder)
1. **PhysX mass unit = 1e5 kg** (`body_tuning.sim_mass_unit_kg`). Measured: a single 1e6 kg ArticulationBody link falls through static ground under PGS; 1e7 kg sinks under TGS; a 1e7 kg Rigidbody is fine. Cause is very likely PhysX's articulation contact response floor / CFM (unit response below about 1e-5 is treated as zero). Scaling mass, inertia, forces and gains by one factor leaves the physics identical. All contract and log values are SI. **This is project-wide**: every body in a PhysX scene must use the same unit (kaiju, buildings, debris).
2. **Vectors in contracts are `System.Numerics`**, so contracts stay engine-free and `IntentFrame.target` is literally a `Vector3`.
3. **Config loading without a JSON library**: data specs are plain serialisable classes whose field names mirror the JSON keys; Unity loads them with `JsonUtility`, `dotnet test` with `System.Text.Json`.
4. **`IBodyWorld` in Physics.Body.Contracts**: units create bodies through it, so `Units.Mech` runtime assemblies reference no implementation assembly. Composition roots (Scenarios, Editor, tests) are separate assemblies.
5. **Headless runs use `Physics.simulationMode = Script` in edit mode** from `-executeMethod` (0.7 s wall per 30 s simulated). PlayMode tests use the same stepping. The mode is restored after every run because Unity writes it back to `DynamicsManager.asset` on quit.
6. **Project settings changed** (data): TGS solver, enhanced determinism, improved patch friction (R-005 R3, R15, R16) by `PhysicsProjectSettings.Apply`; `CoreSettings.json` fixed step 0.02 -> 1/60 s; `TimeManager.asset` fixed timestep to match (otherwise Core's `Adapter_EnterPlayModeTwice...` test reports a mismatch). Core's generated `GameCoreConfig.asset` picked up the three new flag files (its own generator did that at editor boot).
7. **Step record index**: n = state after n physics steps; events of step n are written after record n.
8. **Shove applied on the pelvis at whole-body CoM height.** At torso height (60 m) a 0.3 m g push is equivalent to 0.42 m g at the CoM and cannot be held by any ankle strategy.
9. **Height loop at 0.5 Hz** (R-005 suggests 0.3 Hz): 0.3 Hz walked less stably in the sweep.
10. **Logs are committed gzip-compressed** (225 MB raw).
11. **Tangential foot force is estimated**, because Unity's contact points carry the normal impulse only.
12. `gait_log` positions: CoM and sole centres computed in float64 from float32 link states (T-002's validator rejects float32-quantised positions; the engine has no float64 positions to give at M0).

## What the engine does that the documentation does not say (all measured, `evidence/T-005/probes`)
- `ArticulationDrive.targetVelocity` is in **degrees per second**, not rad/s.
- Spherical joint coordinates are the **rotation vector**; joint forces act about the **child** link's axes.
- `jointForce` persists until rewritten.
- `dofCount` reads 3 for a spherical joint with one axis locked, but the reduced data are compact (2 values); assigning a 2-value `ArticulationReducedSpace` works in edit mode and raises an engine assertion in play mode, so the adapter sizes the struct by `dofCount` and fills the compact slots.
- Half period of a known spring-inertia: 1.000 s under PGS, 0.933 s under TGS (expected 1.0): TGS drives are about 15 % stiffer than the formula.
- Default ArticulationBody damping is 0.05 / 0.05 and joint friction 0.05.
- Articulation links never collide with each other by default.

## Diagnosis of the walk (what limits it)
1. **Reach geometry against the trunk height loop.** With flat feet and hips near standing height, a step longer than about 14 m cannot be reached (leg 38 m). The controller lowers the hips by geometry for longer steps, which bends the stance knee to 40 to 50 degrees (knee torque near its 8e8 N m limit) and pushes the ankle past its 20 degree dorsiflexion range, so the limit stop, not the controller, then sets the centre of pressure. A real long step needs heel-off and toe-off (the trailing foot rolling over its toe) and a hip that vaults; the controller keeps both feet flat. This is the main reason speed is capped at Froude 0.02.
2. **Swing-leg reaction.** Each leg is 20 % of the mass. Swinging it in 2.5 s puts 1e8 N m-class yaw and pitch reactions on the trunk; the only yaw authority is the stance foot's friction, so the trunk yaws several degrees per step, feet twist (M-002) and at longer steps the yaw error grows until a foot lands badly. No counter-rotation of the upper body is planned (arms only mirror the legs).
3. **Capture-point prediction error.** Foot placement uses the DCM predicted at touchdown from the commanded CoP; the realised CoP differs when the ankle is at a limit or the load changes quickly, so feet land 3 to 6 m off and the DCM runs ahead or stalls.
4. **Hard touchdown.** Rigid contact and position-driven swing give 4.5 body-weight spikes and GW power spikes when the trunk has sagged below plan. A compliant ankle / knee at touchdown (impedance drop before contact) is missing.
5. **Turning while walking and 180 degrees on the spot** fail for reason 2 plus foot yaw limits per step.

Suggested next cycle, in order: (a) heel-off / toe-off with a planned heel-to-toe CoP and a vaulting hip trajectory; (b) angular-momentum planning for the swing (upper-body counter-rotation through the waist yaw DOF, lower swing accelerations); (c) touchdown impedance; (d) step timing adaptation (not only placement); (e) then re-measure M-002 slip, which should fall with (b). If two more cycles do not reach Froude 0.10, the honest options are a research task on whole-body MPC / learned tracking for this mass distribution, or a founder decision on a declared assist (Q-014).
