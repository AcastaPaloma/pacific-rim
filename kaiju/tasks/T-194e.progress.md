# T-194e: root cause of the p4_d_dolly frame-33 depth tie in the geo/building mesh (dotnet only, no Unity run)

## Finding: the builder does NOT emit a face coplanar with the terrain
Scan of every building render mesh (`BuildingExtruder.Extrude` via `BuildingsLayer`) against the rendered terrain (`GeoHeightField.SampleRender`), all 15 committed L0 tiles, horizontal faces only (|ny| >= 0.99):
- Bottom (floor) faces: 0 of ~190k within 5 cm of the terrain on every tile. On L0_30_36 (the p4_d tile; 2412 buildings, 19392 bottom faces) the closest bottom face is 0.32 m from the terrain. Cause: `bottom = min(base, ground_min) - buildingSkirtM (2.0 m)` (GeoLayers.cs), so floors sit about 2 m below ground. The only buildings with a floor near ground level are `min_height_m > 0` parts (floating floor, 17 on 30_36), not near terrain either.
- Roof faces: 0 within 5 cm on 30_36; one triangle on L0_29_36 is within 8.7 mm (nearly buried roof; not in the p4_d tile).
- Landmarks (`GeoLandmarks`, floor at `BaseNavd88M`, no skirt) are the only extrusions without a skirt; not the renderer pair seen in T-194b (`BuildingChunks/buildings_4_4` vs `Terrain/terrain_4_4`).

So the "coplanar footprint bottom face" premise of T-194c/d is not supported by the mesh data. No source change is justified: removing or offsetting faces would alter output for no measured benefit, and the footprint face is not the problem. The `MeshCollider` per building (`GeoTileInstance`, uses `b.Mesh`) and the destruction exporter (uses footprint polygons, not the mesh) were checked; the floor is kept for the collider (closed prism) regardless.

## Most likely real cause (not proven; needs a Unity run)
The two tied surfaces at pixel (109,188) are a building WALL and the terrain: wall bottoms pass through the terrain surface (2 m skirt), so a wall/terrain crease line exists on every building and a pixel on that crease has near-equal depth from two different primitives (rasteriser/early-Z tie, per-process state). Lifting buildings 0.02 m / sinking terrain 0.02 m moves the crease off that pixel, which is why it works. A source fix would need walls not to cross the terrain at all (stop at ground, which changes the look at slopes) and is out of scope here.

## Delivered
- `Assets/Game/World/Geo/Tests/EditMode/GeoBuildingTerrainCoplanarTests.cs` (runs in dotnet `DotnetTests/WorldGeo.Tests` and Unity EditMode): bottom faces never within 5 cm of terrain, roof faces never within 5 cm (tile 30_36), every skirt-built extrusion reaches `min(base,ground_min) - skirt`. Guards against a future builder change introducing a coplanar face. Full Geo dotnet suite: 76 pass, 0 fail (log: run `dotnet test DotnetTests/WorldGeo.Tests`).
- Builder and capture sink flag unchanged; default output bit-identical.

## How a later Unity run verifies with sink 0 (T-194d method, do not run now)
1. 4 sequential separate Unity batch processes of `p4_d_dolly_v4` (regular path set, `-captureScale 4,8 -captureMaxFrames 60`) with `-captureTerrainSink 0`, keeping `-captureLiftM 0.02` (and then a second batch with `-captureLiftM 0` too).
2. `capture.py xproc --a <rootN> --b <rootM>` pairwise (and compare `hashes.sha256` md5 prefixes). Expected if the wall/terrain-crease theory holds: sink 0 and lift 0 reproduce two outcomes (frame 33 x4, one pixel); if instead the runs are identical, the tie was transient and the sink can be dropped. Optionally use `KAIJU_CAPTURE_DIAG=<dir> KAIJU_DIAG_COVER=1` to confirm the two covering renderers' faces at the pixel are wall vs terrain.
