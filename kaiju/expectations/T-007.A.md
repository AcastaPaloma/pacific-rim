# T-007 expectations · draft A (expect-A, 2026-11-14)
IDs: D-007, D-008, D-009, D-011, D-022, D-023, D-025, F-001 · metrics M-001, M-003, M-006, M-008 · pillars P1, P4.
R-002, R-003 and R-005 were not available when this was drafted. Numbers below are founder-intent windows. For joint positions and segment lengths, R-005 wins; any conflict between R-005 and a window here is reported in `EVIDENCE.md`, never resolved silently.
Conventions: H = rest-pose height, sole to top of head shell. Widths are measured on the front orthographic silhouette in rest pose (arms hanging, feet at the R-005 neutral stance). "Mech's left" = L.

## 1. Acceptance criteria
**Originality (D-009, D-022)**
- AC-1 Prompt hygiene: no franchise, studio, artist, character, product or named-design term in any prompt in the prompt sheet or `PROVENANCE.md`. The evaluator checks against its own private term list (not committed) and reads every prompt. Pass = zero hits.
- AC-2 No style reference, and no third-party image, was uploaded or used as an image prompt. The only allowed image prompts are this task's own earlier outputs, each cited by job ID.
- AC-3 Blind resemblance test: eval-style views the front, side and 3/4 renders and the 24/48 px silhouettes. Working from its own knowledge of well-known giant-robot designs in film, TV, animation and games, it must not be able to name a specific existing design that the head, chest, shoulder construction, colour scheme or overall silhouette recalls. One named resemblance fails that element. The same check runs against the subjects of the style refs: no horns, visored or skull knight helms, capes, fur, tusks or riders.
- AC-4 No genre-signature identifiers on the greybox or on any kept concept: no V-shaped crest or antenna fins, no glowing round chest core, no human-like face, eyes-plus-mouth grille or teeth, and no emblem, text, number or insignia.
- AC-5 Own identity: a short design note names 2–3 signature forms in plain words (for example head shape, shoulder construction, shin armour). The evaluator finds each of them in the concept turnaround and on the greybox at 48 px.

**Silhouette at pixel scale (P4, M-003).** Test: orthographic front, side and 3/4 views, unlit black on flat white, downscaled with nearest-neighbour to H = 180, 48 and 24 px. 180 px is about full frame height at 1/6 internal resolution; 24–48 px is the gameplay distance.
- AC-6 At 48 px front: head, two shoulder masses, two arms each separated from the torso by a ≥ 1 px gap along ≥ 40% of the arm's length, a ≥ 1 px crotch gap, and two feet wider than the shanks above them, all distinct. At 48 px side: head, chest/back mass, leg, and toe and heel projecting past the shank.
- AC-7 At 24 px front it still reads as a humanoid giant: the head is a visible bump narrower than the shoulders, the shoulders are the widest mass, and both legs are separate. Three independent evaluator looks (blind, alongside 2 decoy silhouettes) correctly point to head, arms and legs.
- AC-8 At 180 px with flat palette colours, the plate breakup reads as 3–5 posterised value bands. No geometric feature smaller than 1 m (≈ 2 internal px) carries the read: nothing important is lost when such features are removed.
- AC-9 It reads as a machine, not a scaled person: the 48 px silhouette overlaid on a human silhouette scaled to the same H has IoU ≤ 0.75.

**Heavy, heroic proportions (D-008, P1, M-001).** All values are measured in Blender and reported in `dimensions.json`.
- AC-10 Height and scale markers:
  - H = 80.0 m ± 0.5 m.
  - Top of the torso shell (shoulder line) at 62–70 m. The 67 m deck marker crosses the upper chest; the whole head sits above it.
- AC-11 Upper body:
  - Shoulder width (outer shoulder armour) / H = 0.30–0.40.
  - Shoulder width / hip width ≥ 1.4.
  - Head height / H = 0.05–0.09, and head width / shoulder width ≤ 0.30. A small head sells the scale.
  - Forearm maximum width ≥ 1.1 × upper-arm maximum width.
  - Fist width / H ≥ 0.055. Fists must be believable, with room for forearm hardpoints (D-011).
- AC-12 Legs and stance:
  - Leg length (sole to hip joint centre) / H = 0.44–0.53.
  - Thigh and shank minimum width / H ≥ 0.08: columns, not stilts.
  - Foot length / H = 0.16–0.21 and foot width / H = 0.10–0.14, with foot width / shank width ≥ 1.2.
  - Stance (outer edge to outer edge of the feet) / shoulder width = 0.65–0.90.
- AC-13 Volume-weighted centre of mass height / H = 0.48–0.58.
- AC-14 Panel read on the turnaround with the 1.8 m human and the 67 m marker: "thousands of tonnes, heroic, upright; not lanky, hunched or toy-like" scores ≥ 8/10.

**True scale and mass consistency (D-007, D-008, M-006)**
- AC-15 1 Blender unit = 1 m. Scene unit scale is 1 and all object scales are applied (= 1). H in Unity matches H in Blender within 1 cm.
- AC-16 Sole contact area per foot (ground-touching faces, projected, rest pose) = 150 m² ± 10%, with L and R within 1% of each other.
- AC-17 Every joint position and segment length is within 2% of the config derived from R-005, reported per joint with its deviation.
- AC-18 Every part mesh is closed and manifold. Total enclosed volume is 5,000–10,000 m³ (§4: a scaled human is ≈ 6,100 m³, plus armour bulk). The report gives the implied mass at ρ̄ = 1,000 and 2,500 kg/m³, and that range must overlap 6–15 kt. Each segment's volume fraction is within ±30% (relative) of its R-005 mass fraction. Segments under 2% are exempt; any other deviation is listed with a reason.

**Rigid parts and pivots (F-001)**
- AC-19 Exactly 15 rigid parts: pelvis, torso, head, plus upper arm, forearm, hand, thigh, shank and foot for L and R. Names map 1:1 to the R-005 segment IDs, and the export contains nothing else (helper empties excepted, if named).
- AC-20 Each part's origin is at its proximal joint centre within 0.25 m (the pelvis at the R-005 root). The hierarchy is pelvis → torso → head / upper arm → forearm → hand, and pelvis → thigh → shank → foot.
- AC-21 Range-of-motion test with four poses:
  1. rest
  2. guard, one fist forward at shoulder height
  3. deep crouch, knees ≥ 90°
  4. both arms overhead, shoulder flexion ≥ 150°

  Non-adjacent parts never interpenetrate by more than 0.5 m. Adjacent joints stay visually closed: no daylight through a joint at 48 px.
- AC-22 L and R are mirror-symmetric within 1 cm. Asymmetry comes later through loadouts (D-025).
- AC-23 Budget: ≤ 20k triangles in total, no textures, and ≤ 4 flat material slots named by palette role: primary, secondary, joint, accent (M-008, M-003).

**Concept layers and provenance (D-023)**
- AC-24 `art/concepts/mech_frame_balanced/` holds all four layers, each kept output as a file:
  1. silhouette explorations
  2. orthographic front / side / back turnaround
  3. plate breakup and joint mechanics
  4. mood frame

  It also holds the prompt sheet and `PROVENANCE.md`. For each output, PROVENANCE records the tool, exact prompt, parameters, job ID or link, date, selection rationale and "AI-generated: yes".
- AC-25 PROVENANCE counts ≤ 16 generations in total, discarded ones included, and shows no purchase, settings change or terms acceptance. If Phase A was blocked, it records what blocked it, when, and the fallback, and Phase B is still delivered in full.
- AC-26 Traceability: the greybox front silhouette, overlaid on the chosen turnaround front at the same H, shows the same major masses. IoU is reported (≥ 0.6 expected). Each intentional departure, for example one made to meet AC-11 or R-005, is listed with its reason.

**Unity import**
- AC-27 Import: no console errors or warnings. Y-up, the mech faces +Z, root rotation is identity, every transform scale is 1, and H = 80 m in Unity units.
- AC-28 Reproducibility: the import settings and the prefab under `Assets/Game/Units/Mech/Art/` come from an Editor script, and a second run produces an empty `git diff`. All `.meta` files are committed, and `.blend` and `.fbx` are tracked by LFS.
- AC-29 Segment mapping: the part → segment-ID mapping asset has 15 unique entries, each resolving to a transform in the prefab, with IDs spelled as in R-005. A named EditMode test checks mapping completeness, H, joints against config (2%) and axes/scale, and passes through the Unity CLI with XML output.
- AC-30 Module rules (`MODULARITY.md` §1): dimensions come from Config and are not hard-coded, and the Units.Mech `MODULE.md` lists the new assets and IDs.

## 2. Style references (style only; nothing of their subject matter)
- **Mood frame palette and air:** ref_01 top-right and ref_03 top-right. These give a cold slate-teal / blue-grey family, a giant form whose base dissolves into mist, low-contrast distance, clustered cloud masses with dithered edges, and tiny figures at the base. This is the default for bay water and fog.
- **One warm accent:** as in ref_01's bottom panel (lavender-blue with peach glow and vertical light streaks) or ref_04 (slate with a single crimson accent).
- **Value structure:** ref_01 top-left and ref_05. The near giant is the darkest mass against a lighter, hazier sky, with a low horizon and a large sky.
- **Scale figures:** ref_03 top-left and ref_05. The mood frame must show true-scale human objects in the foreground: people, cars, boats or port cranes.
- **Posterised flat value bands:** ref_04 and ref_03 bottom-right.
- **Ordered dither and pixel grid:** ref_06 (dither texture only) and ref_01 bottom (pixel grid).
- **Check on the mood frame:** k-means with 8 clusters. ≥ 90% of pixels fall in ≤ 3 hue families plus one accent; the accent is ≤ 10% of pixels; mean sky luminance is above mean mech luminance; and the water line sits at the ankle to mid-shin (the Bay is a few metres deep).
- **In-engine:** flat slate-family materials with ≤ 1 accent. Under the pixel proxy (AC-8), the near mech is the darkest value band.

## 3. What would disappoint them
- It looks like any famous giant robot, or like a knight or beast from the fan-art references.
- The first impression is genre cliché: a glowing chest reactor, a V-crest, or a face.
- It is lanky, skinny, hunched or toy-like: a big head, small feet, or legs that could not plausibly carry 10,000 t.
- At 24–48 px it is a blob: arms merge with the torso, the legs fuse, the head disappears.
- Greeble noise that shimmers at pixel scale, or the opposite: a smooth mannequin with no plate breakup.
- Scale faked anywhere: 1 unit ≠ 1 m, an import scale fudge, Unity height not 80 m, origins at world zero, or a Z-up model lying on its back in Unity.
- One merged mesh, pivots away from the joints, or shells that collide at modest angles, so the physics body can't crouch, guard or raise its arms.
- Provenance gaps: unlogged or extra generations, prompts paraphrased instead of exact, a name in a prompt, or an uploaded reference.
- A glossy, full-colour sci-fi mood frame with no haze, no water and no tiny figures, or one that copies a reference's composition.
- A hand-edited prefab, a non-reproducible import, numbers copied by hand instead of read from config, or a requirement silently dropped.
