# T-007 · Expectations, draft B (expect-B)

Task: first original mech frame, Balanced chassis (D-025), as concept layers (D-023), an 80 m greybox split into rigid parts (F-001), and a Unity prefab.
Viewpoints: (a) a player seeing this machine for the first time in a style frame, knee-deep in the Bay beside real buildings; (b) the engineer who will later hang ArticulationBody joints, locational damage (D-012, F-007) and swappable limbs and weapons (D-025, F-006) on it.
Inputs read: T-007 brief, GAME_BREAKDOWN §1–§4, §9, §10, MODULARITY, STACK, AGENT_PROTOCOL, `art/style_refs/` (style only). `research/R-005.md`, `R-002.md` and `R-003.md` did not exist when this was written; criteria that depend on them say so.

## 1. Acceptance criteria (each checkable from artifacts in `evidence/T-007/`)

**A · Weight and mass that you can read from the forms (P1, D-008, M-001, M-006)**
1. **It reads as thousands of tonnes, not as a lean robot.** In the side and front orthos, the legs are columns: the narrowest width of the thigh ≥ 0.10 H (8 m) and of the shank ≥ 0.08 H; nowhere is a load-bearing limb thinner at mid-length than at its joints (no "chicken legs", no hourglass shins). A blind panel of 3 evaluators shown the 1/6-resolution silhouette rates "looks extremely heavy" ≥ 7/10 (eval-style records the scores).
2. **The visual mass is where the physics says it is.** The dimensions report gives the volume-weighted centroid of the whole closed body. Its height is within ±3% H of the CoM height in R-005, or within 0.50–0.60 H if R-005 is missing (flag which). In front view, the centroid lies on the mid-plane (left/right volume difference < 1%).
3. **Heroic but planted proportions (D-008).** Shoulder span / hip span is in 1.4–2.0; the torso plus shoulders carry the visual bulk, but the stance width (outer foot edges) is ≥ the shoulder span × 0.7, so the figure does not look like it would topple. The head is small relative to the chest (head height ≤ 0.10 H).
4. **Total volume and implied mass are consistent with §4.** Every part is a closed (watertight) mesh. Total enclosed volume × the config density gives 6,000–15,000 t. Per-segment volume fractions are within ±30% (relative) of R-005 mass fractions under the stated density per segment; every larger deviation is explained (for example a hollow shoulder housing).
5. **Feet built for ground pressure.** Each sole has a contact area of 150 m² ± 10% (computed from the sole polygon, not the bounding box), a flat, horizontal sole coplanar with Z = 0 in rest pose (± 0.05 m), a length/width ratio of 1.3–2.2, and the ankle joint centre above the sole within 15% of foot length of the sole's area centroid. The report prints static pressure m·g/A per foot for the config mass (≈ 650 kPa at 10,000 t, §4). No stiletto heels, claws or narrow toes that would make one footprint read as a point load.
6. **Joints and actuators you believe could move this mass.** Each of hip, knee, ankle, shoulder, elbow, wrist and neck has a visible housing form (drum, ball or yoke) centred on the joint pivot (pivot inside the housing volume), and knees and elbows show at least one actuator or tendon form that spans the joint on the side that would pull. Load paths are continuous: from the side view, a straight line from each shoulder housing to the hip housing runs through solid structure, not empty air.
7. **Range of motion without self-intersection.** With parts rotated about their pivots to the target ranges (hip flexion −30° to +100°, knee 0–120°, ankle ±30°, shoulder flexion −40° to +170°, elbow 0–140°, neck yaw ±60°; R-005 ranges override these if they exist), the measured overlap volume between neighbouring parts is ≤ 2% of the smaller part, and no part passes through a non-neighbour. Report per joint.
8. **Fists made for punching (D-011).** Each hand part is a closed-fist block with a flat striking face of ≥ 6 m² across the knuckles, and the forearm is at least as wide as the fist (a punch's load path looks continuous).

**B · Scale cues (D-007, §3, §9 composition)**
9. Height to the top of the head is 80.0 m ± 0.5 m in Blender and in Unity (bounds read back in both).
10. The 67 m bridge-deck marker crosses the torso part's bounding box (chest at deck height) and the head stays below 88 m; the 1.8 m human figure stands at the foot and is visibly about one toe-cap high (the foot is ≥ 7× the human's height in length).
11. **Readability at pixel scale (P4, M-003).** Downsampled to 1/4 and 1/6 of a 1080p frame with the mech filling 80% of the frame height, the threshold silhouette still separates head, both shoulders, both arms from the torso, and both legs (gap between legs visible in front view). The same at 40 px tall (gameplay distance): head and shoulders still read as a mech, not as a building. No modelled feature is smaller than 1.0 m.

**C · Modular frame and later damage (D-025, F-001, F-007, D-012)**
12. **Exactly one rigid part per R-005 segment**: pelvis, torso, head, upper arm L/R, forearm L/R, hand L/R, thigh L/R, shank L/R, foot L/R (15 parts unless R-005 says otherwise), no loose extra meshes, no part spanning a joint.
13. **Names are stable, machine-parseable and match segment IDs**: one naming scheme with side suffix (for example `Balanced_ForearmL`) used identically in the `.blend`, the `.fbx`, the Unity hierarchy and the mapping asset. A test lists all 15 names and fails on any missing, duplicated or unmapped part.
14. **Pivots at joint centres.** Each part's origin is at its proximal joint centre (pelvis: its own centre per R-005) within 2% of segment length of the R-005 / config value; segment lengths (joint-to-joint) are within 2% of the config (brief constraint). Left/right parts are exact mirrors (pivot positions mirrored within 1 cm).
15. **Kinematic tree in Unity matches the body**: pelvis is the root; torso → head and → upper arms → forearms → hands; pelvis → thighs → shanks → feet. Rotating one part in the editor moves only its children.
16. **Swappable limbs show their seams.** At each limb root (shoulder, hip) and at each forearm hardpoint there is a visible mounting interface in the concept and a named socket transform in the prefab. Left and right sockets of the same kind have identical local geometry, so a Heavy Brawler limb could plug into the same socket. A swap test replaces one forearm with a stand-in part via the mapping/socket data alone, without editing any other part.
17. **Colliders can be derived.** For each part, convex-hull volume / mesh volume ≤ 1.6 (or the part is split into ≤ 3 named convex pieces), so physics shapes stay close to what the player sees.
18. **Plate breakup exists as design**: the concept layer 3 sheet shows the armour plates, their seams and which plates belong to which segment, so later locational damage (plates, joints, limbs failing separately) has a visual plan. The greybox itself may keep one material slot per part (see Q-NEW-c).

**D · Originality, provenance, Unity**
19. **Original IP (D-009, D-022).** A side-by-side review by eval-style against well-known giant-robot designs finds no recognisable head, chest, colour scheme or emblem. Specific bans: head crests, fins or horns in a V; a glowing round core as the chest's centrepiece; national-flag or racing-stripe livery; any emblem, lettering or number on the hull; anything copied from the subject matter of the style refs (horns, fur, capes, banners, helmets, tusks).
20. **Clean prompts.** A grep of the prompt sheet and `PROVENANCE.md` against a banned-terms list (franchise, studio, artist, character, product names) returns nothing; prompts describe the look in plain words; no image was uploaded to any service.
21. **Provenance complete.** Every kept concept image has: tool, exact prompt, parameters, job ID or link, date, selection rationale, `AI-generated: yes`. Generations used ≤ 16, counted. If Phase A stopped (login wall, payment, CAPTCHA, moderation), the stop and its screen are recorded and Phase B proceeded from the written prompt sheet.
22. **Concept layers are coherent.** Four layers exist: (1) silhouettes (several distinct options, one chosen with reasons), (2) front/side/back turnaround of the chosen design with matching heights and joint lines, (3) plate breakup and joint mechanics, (4) mood frame. The greybox follows the chosen turnaround (eval-style overlays the side ortho and the Blender side render at equal height).
23. **Unity import is correct and reproducible.** Y-up, 1 unit = 1 m, no scale fix-up on the root, no import warnings in the log; the prefab and the part→segment mapping asset are created by an Editor script, and running it twice gives the same result; every added asset and folder has a `.meta`; `.blend`/`.fbx` are in LFS; flat material colours come from a palette profile, no textures with detail below 1 m; total triangles ≤ 20,000.

## 2. Style references (style only; no subject matter)
- **Mood frame composition** (layer 4): match the giant-figure panels of ref_01 (top-right, bottom), ref_03 (top-left, top-right) and ref_05: low horizon, large sky, the giant filling most of the frame height, legs sinking into mist or water, **tiny human-scale figures and objects in the foreground** (here: people, cars, a boat, a pier crane), haze making far layers lighter and lower in contrast.
- **Palette**: 2–3 dominant hues + one accent, per §9. Recommended: the slate-teal / slate-blue family of ref_01 top-right and ref_03 top-right (cold Bay morning), or slate with a single crimson accent as in ref_04 and ref_03 bottom-right; the warm rust-red/orange-sepia of ref_01 top-left and ref_03 top-left as the alternative. The mech's own colour is a muted mid-value body colour plus at most one accent; it must stay readable against the haze.
- **Pixel treatment**: posterised value bands and hard-edged shading planes as in ref_01 bottom and ref_05; ordered dithering in sky, fog and smoke as in ref_06 and the cloud masses of ref_01 bottom; vertical light shafts allowed (ref_01 bottom).
- **In-engine capture**: flat palette colours, 3–4 value bands; it need not run the final pixel pipeline, but a downsampled (1/4, 1/6) version must show that the silhouette and plate seams survive.

## 3. What would disappoint the founders
- A slender, athletic robot with thin shins and small feet that looks like a 2 m figure scaled up: no sense of tonnes (P1).
- Anything that makes a reviewer think of a famous robot or of the reference images' characters: crests, horns, glowing chest discs, flag colours, capes, helmets (D-009).
- A pretty mood frame whose mech has a different design from the turnaround and the greybox; or a mood frame with no scale cue, so the mech could be 8 m tall.
- Feet shaped like boots or claws whose area is far from 150 m², or a model whose sole floats above or cuts into the ground.
- One fused mesh, parts split in the wrong places, pivots at the world origin or the part centre, names that differ between Blender and Unity: the physics engineer has to redo it.
- Joints that are just two boxes touching: no housing, nothing that looks like it carries a load, parts that clip at modest angles.
- Fine greebles, panel lines or decals that turn into noise at 1/6 resolution.
- Hand-edited `.prefab` text, a prefab that cannot be regenerated, Z-up leaking into Unity (mech lying on its back), a 0.01 or 100× scale.
- Missing or vague provenance; any franchise or artist name in a prompt; more than 16 generations; any purchase, settings change or term accepted.
