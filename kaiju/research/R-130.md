## 0. Summary (the answer in ten lines)
1. **Gait band.** Kaiju walk Fr 0.02–0.25. Cruise is Fr 0.08 ± 0.02 for the quadruped (elephant metabolic optimum Fr 0.09 [S5]) and 0.10 ± 0.03 for the bipeds. A no-flight "amble/charge" runs from 0.25 to a hard cap of 0.5. There is never a whole-body aerial phase [S3][S4]. At 80 m this is 2.7–9.4 m/s walking (up to 13–15 m/s charging), a stride period of 5–11 s and a stride frequency of 0.09–0.20 Hz **[D]**.
2. **Duty factor.** The quadruped needs β ≥ 0.75 to be **statically stable** through a lateral-sequence walk. The script reproduces the McGhee–Frank result [M7], but only with the 100 m² pads modelled. With point feet the margin is negative at every β **[D]**. Above Fr ≈ 0.08, β falls below 0.75 and balance becomes dynamic (QP/DCM).
3. **Foot loads.** These are the peaks of one foot's stance profile, not the sum over the feet. Quadruped at cruise: fore 0.63 BW = 62 MN, hind 0.42 BW. Biped at Fr 0.10: 1.22 BW = 119 MN **[D]**. Whole-body vertical force ripple: quadruped 0.91–1.05 BW, biped 0.79–1.22 BW **[D]**. Graviportal quadrupeds are much smoother, which is exactly what T-005 lacked (§6).
4. **Pads.** Geometrically scaled elephant pads would press 1.28 MPa. Oversized pads are needed: 100 m² per quadruped foot (294 kPa static, 618 kPa walking peak) and 211 m² per biped foot (465 kPa single stance) **[A]/[D]**. On Bay mud and soft clay the quasi-static punching depth exceeds 6–30 m, so a layered soil model is mandatory. Sand and stiff clay carry the load with no punching **[D]**.
5. **Tail.** In the horizontal biped (T) a 25 % tail halves the static hip torque (6.0e8 against 1.19e9 N·m) and puts the CoM 0.14 l ahead of the hip **[D]**. In the upright biped (B) an 18 % tail pulls the CoM 2.4 m *behind* the hip, so the body must lean about 20° forward or carry tail load on the ground (tripod) **[D]**.
6. **Tail as yaw actuator.** Tail/body yaw inertia is 3.3 (B), 1.06 (T) and 0.16 (Q). A ±40° tail sweep turns the body 61° / 41° / 11° with zero foot moment. A 1e9 N·m tail-base torque is 4.6× the stance-foot free-moment budget **[D]**, so the tail is the turning actuator that protects M-002 (no foot twist).
7. **Stability ceilings.** Biped single-support lateral ceiling 0.15 g (B) and 0.11 g (T). Backward ankle ceiling 0.175 g; tripod mode raises it to 1.25 g. LIPM time constant 1.9–2.2 s **[D]**.
8. **Controller.** The T-005 path transfers: a whole-body / centroidal QP tracking an offline-optimised reference. Joint replay does not (round 3). A dense full-ID QP for the quadruped is n ≈ 124, about 2.4 ms per tick (extrapolated from T-005's measured solver). A centroidal force QP is n = 24–48, 0.07–0.3 ms **[D]**. So the quadruped uses a centroidal QP plus per-leg Jᵀ / inverse dynamics.
9. **RL.** The policy commands a `LocomotionIntent` (velocity, yaw rate, gait mode, tail mode) at about 2 Hz and reads a `LocomotionEnvelope` back. It never commands torques (§8).
10. **Gating.** All offline work (Python plant, reference optimisation, 3D centroidal prototype, envelope surrogate, C# engine-free code under dotnet) can start now. Unity work is gated on T-005 9.5 (a stable QP walk in Unity) or a founder ruling (`tasks/T-160.md`).

**What it means for a 10,000 t kaiju.**
- **No flight, ever.** Elephants at 4–5 t already avoid it [S3]. Our bodies are 2,000× heavier.
- **Slow cruise.** Animals near the elephant's optimum Fr 0.09 read as heavy; sauropod trackways suggest Fr < 0.1 [S2].
- **Column limbs.** Upright posture keeps the GRF line close to the joints [M11], so joint torques stay at mech scale even though the loads are larger (§4.4).

Readings:
- β ≥ 0.75 is the static threshold [M7], reproduced.

- Four-foot stance: lateral ceiling 0.9 × 12 / 34 = 0.32 g **[D]**.

Sizing rules, taken from the table:
1. **T (horizontal).** Choose the tail fraction so the CoM sits 0.10–0.15 l ahead of the hip; 25–32 % at these front proportions. The tail halves the hip pitch torque, which saves actuator margin for brawling.
2. **B (upright).** A heavy tail is a liability statically: the CoM goes behind the hip. Give the pose a 15–20° forward lean, or let the tail carry load (tripod) when standing. The trajectory optimiser decides the lean, not the animator.
3. **Yaw.** With R ≥ 1 the tail can reorient the body by tens of degrees without twisting the planted feet. The tail yaw task must be in the QP, and its torque must be rate-limited so the body never exceeds the M-001 yaw limits (M-001.x yaw accel scaled to the kaiju; 143 °/s² is about 36× the mech's 4 °/s² cap).
4. **Q.** A small tail (R = 0.16) is decorative for balance. It still logs contacts (tail swipes, §5).

Body pitch time constants are similar to the mech's (R-005 §7: 8.2 s to topple from 5°). A mid-stride biped that loses balance has ≈ 2 s of DCM divergence time per e-fold **[D]**. A Q in 4-foot stance cannot topple sideways below a 0.32 g lateral load **[D]**.

## 4. Ground reaction forces, pressure, sinkage
### 4.1 Per-foot loads **[D]**
Half-sine stance profiles whose stride average equals the foot's static share. Biped walking GRF in humans is 1.0–1.5 BW [R-005 refs 31, 32], consistent with this.

| | static per foot | cruise peak per foot | whole-body vertical force ripple at cruise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q fore | 0.30 BW = 29.4 MN | 0.63 BW = 61.8 MN (Fr 0.08) | 0.91–1.05 BW |
| Q hind | 0.20 BW = 19.6 MN | 0.42 BW = 41.2 MN | (same) |
| B / T | 1.00 BW = 98 MN single stance | 1.22 BW = 119 MN (Fr 0.10) | 0.79–1.22 BW |
| at Fr 0.50 (cap) | | Q fore 0.91 BW; biped 1.51 BW | Q 0.71–1.12; biped **0.18–1.51** |

The Q fore value at the cap (0.91 BW) stays under the elephant's "< 1 BW per forelimb" [S6].

The biped at Fr 0.5 dips to 0.18 BW of total vertical force: within a hair of an aerial phase. That is the reason for the 0.5 cap [A]. Above it a 10,000 t biped would be "running", which the brief forbids.

### 4.2 Pressure and quasi-static punching **[D]**
Terzaghi square-footing bearing capacity q_u = 1.3 c N_c + γ D N_q + 0.4 γ B N_γ, with Terzaghi factors [M12]. "Punching depth" is the smallest D with q_u ≥ p. It is quasi-static and single-layer, a game-tuning curve, not geotechnics. Soil values are [A]: Bay mud c 10 kPa; soft clay 25; stiff clay 100; loose sand φ 30°; dense sand φ 34°.

| case | p (kPa) | Bay mud | soft clay | stiff clay | loose sand | dense sand |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q fore static, 100 m² | 294 | 14.7 m | 6.4 m | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Q fore cruise peak, 100 m² | 618 | > 30 m | 25.5 m | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| biped single stance, 211 m² | 465 | 26 m | 16.5 m | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| biped cruise peak, 211 m² | 566 | > 30 m | 22.4 m | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Q fore with a geometrically scaled elephant pad, 23 m² | 1,280 | > 30 | > 30 | 28 m | 1.7 m | 0 |

Readings:
- **Sand is not the problem.** Large footings on frictional soil gain capacity with width: surface q_u is 1.3–4.0 MPa at B = 10–15 m.
- **Soft cohesive soil is.** Bay mud cannot hold a kaiju foot. In reality the mud is a finite layer over stiffer soil [A, to source in R-131], so the soil model must be layered (sink to the stiff layer, then bear). Otherwise the kaiju walks on the bottom of a 30 m hole.
- **Oversized pads are necessary** [A]. Geometric pads give 1.28 MPa and fail even stiff clay.
- **Locomotion implication.** Sinkage adds a large, lossy vertical compliance. That cuts both ways:
  - good for the T-005 bounce failure mode (§6.3): the soil absorbs landing;
  - bad for timing: lift-off must first extract the foot. The pull-out (suction) force is a soil-model parameter [A].
  - The controller must observe actual pad depth (contact height) and not assume flat rigid ground.

### 4.3 Ground-response contract hook (proposal; Physics.Contact owns the model)
- `ISoilQuery.At(x, z) → SoilClass` (layer stack: c, φ, γ, thickness).
- An engine-free `SoilResponse.Punch(soil, padWidthM, pressurePa) → depthM` and `SoilResponse.Stiffness(...)` (T-170).
- In Unity a pad's ground collider is lowered by the computed depth through a deformable-terrain service. This is a world-module concern; the body never moves ground.
- Sinkage work (F × depth) is logged as dissipated energy so M-006-style energy checks stay closed.

| Q, 0.08 | 61.8 MN | 6.7e7 N·m | 1.8e8 | 3.3e8 |

## 5. Footfall seismic coupling (F-009 hook) **[D]**
- Footfall energy = touchdown kinetic energy of the engaged mass + sinkage work.
- Equivalent magnitude from Gutenberg–Richter, log₁₀ E_s = 1.5 M + 4.8 [M13], with seismic efficiency η [A] (fraction radiated as waves).

| case | vertical impulse | energy | M at η 1e-3 / 1e-2 / 1e-1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q fore on stiff ground (v_td 0.6 m/s, sink 5 cm) | 0.9 MN·s | 2.2 MJ | −1.0 / −0.3 / +0.4 |
| Q fore on soft clay (sink 1 m) | 0.9 MN·s | 38 MJ | −0.1 / +0.5 / +1.2 |
| biped on stiff ground (v_td 1.0 m/s) | 2.5 MN·s | 5.2 MJ | −0.7 / −0.1 / +0.6 |
| biped on Bay mud (sink 3 m) | 2.5 MN·s | 237 MJ | +0.4 / +1.1 / +1.7 |
| B tail slam (6 m/s, 18 % mass) | 10.8 MN·s | 42 MJ | −0.1 / +0.6 / +1.2 |

Every footfall is a microquake of M −1 to +1.7: locally felt at the top of the range, recorded by instruments at the bottom. This gives F-009 and F-020 (emergence earthquakes) a common, physically scaled currency.

The event contract already exists:
- `FootContact` is published in the contact step;
- `FootLoaded` (peak force, impulse) follows when the loading window closes;
- foot index and count are data (MODULE.md).

So no new bus event is needed for the kaiju to drive F-009. Two things are proposed, both as change requests:
- **a payload extension:** `FootLoaded@v2` gains `SinkDepthM`, `DissipatedJ` and `SeismicMagnitude` (computed by the soil service, η from config);
- **tail contacts** (a "foot" of kind Tail).

Camera impulse and audio scale with `FootLoaded`, not `FootContact`, as the module already prescribes.

### 6.2 What the T-005 evidence says not to do (lessons, with their sources)
1. **No joint-space replay.** Round 3, 8.5: every walk fell; half speed, weak swing-knee tracking, yaw drift. Start at the QP layer.
2. **Contact transitions must be dynamically feasible over several nodes.** 9.3b-2's binding constraint was a one-node toe-off/push-off spike (Σ fn 2.9–3.9 m g, qdd off by 54 rad/s²) that no unilateral contact can realise. Kaiju optimisation therefore has, as hard constraints:
   - force-rate bounds |ḟ_n| ≤ k_f m g per second [A: k_f = 1.0];
   - load and unload ramps over ≥ 3 nodes;
   - pad velocity consistent with load: a loaded pad has zero normal velocity relative to the deformed ground.
3. **Landing bounce on a stiff spring contact** (9.3b-2: 2.3–3.1 m g peaks, the stance foot bounces, the fall starts there).
   - Touchdown pad velocity ≤ 0.6 m/s (Q) and ≤ 1.0 m/s (bipeds) [A] (R-005 Q-NEW-c).
   - The controller models pad compliance (spring-aware force prediction), and the soil supplies real damping.
4. **The friction law matters.** The 9.3b-2 plant had no static friction (tanh), so loaded points crept. The kaiju plant uses a stiction-capable law, and the controller commands μ_eff = μ_d/1.5 (M-002 factor).
5. **Planar prototypes hide yaw and lateral failures.** Round-3 yaw drift appeared only in Unity. A quadruped's support polygon is 3D anyway, so the kaiju prototype is 3D from the first step: a centroidal single-rigid-body model with leg kinematics [M14][M15].
6. **The prototype plant must match PhysX contact behaviour.** Round 4 needed a plant of its own because Unity's lag and sag were not explained. The kaiju plant brackets PhysX with two contact variants (compliant spring, near-rigid), and the controller must pass both.
7. **Multi-body determinism.** A second stepping body breaks bit-identity (MODULE.md known limit, C20). Kaiju packs make this worse; determinism tests in §8.4.

### 6.4 Controller cost (extrapolated from T-005 step 9.2: n = 16 → 27 µs, n = 32 → 125 µs, exponent 2.2) **[D]**
| QP | n | estimated warm-solve time |
|---|---|---|
| mech planar full-ID (prototype) | 17 | 0.03 ms |
| mech 3D full-ID | 61 | 0.51 ms |
| biped kaiju 3D full-ID | 67 | 0.63 ms |
| quad kaiju 3D full-ID | 124 | **2.4 ms** |
| quad centroidal wrench QP (4 pads × 6) | 24 | 0.07 ms |
| quad centroidal point-force QP (16 points × 3) | 48 | 0.30 ms |

At 60 Hz with 3 kaiju and 2 mechs, full-ID for the quadruped would use about 7 ms of a 16.7 ms frame on physics control alone.

**Decision (Q-254 and T-164 design):** two stages.
1. A centroidal QP distributes the pad wrenches, tail force and CoM/momentum tasks.
2. A per-limb mapping follows: τ_leg = −J_legᵀ f + leg inverse dynamics (the legs are near-independent columns; their mass is 6 % each [A]), plus null-space posture PD.

Full-ID stays the upgrade path for bipeds, where 0.63 ms is affordable. This is the R-009 "centroidal QP + Jᵀ + null-space" option. For the mech it was the first choice, and 9.3b moved to full-ID because of the planar toe-off problem. For Q the transitions are smooth (§6.3), so the two-stage form is expected to be enough; T-166 tests that.

## 7. Structural damage coupling (F-024)
- The damage module writes `LimbCapability { LimbIndex, TorqueScale 0..1, RomScale 0..1, Detached }`.
- The controller reads it every tick. Torque rows in the QP scale by it, and a detached limb leaves the contact set. Masses come from the articulation at runtime (as in R-005 §5.1).
- Gait fallbacks are data in `kaiju_gait@v1`:
  - Q minus one leg → a 3-leg walk (dog-like, β ≥ 0.83 [A], tail-assisted if any);
  - B minus one leg → tail-tripod crawl, or Fallen;
  - T minus one leg → Fallen, then GetUp attempts on arms and tail.
- The envelope (§8) shrinks accordingly, so the RL policy sees a crippled kaiju as slower and less stable, and learns to retreat. That serves F-022's penalty term.

## 8. RL policy → locomotion layer (M-007, T-041), sim-to-3D and determinism
### 8.1 The command boundary
The policy (F-021 tactical layer) never outputs joint targets or torques. It writes at a fixed decision rate of 2 Hz (every 30 physics steps at 60 Hz) [A]:
`LocomotionIntent { VelocityHeading (vx, vy m/s, heading frame), YawRate (rad/s), Gait (Stand | Walk | Amble | Brace | Tripod | TurnInPlace | Crouch), BodyHeightOffsetM, BodyPitchOffsetRad, Tail (Balance | Sweep | Slam + target), Urgency 0..1 }`

The locomotion controller:
- clamps the intent to the envelope;
- ramps it with the M-001-style kaiju limits (acceleration, yaw rate and yaw acceleration, start and stop steps);
- chooses steps and footholds (Raibert/DCM-style on the reference), contact schedule and tail action;
- solves the QP every physics tick.

It publishes `LocomotionStatus` for the policy's observation:
- `Envelope` (v_max forward/back/lateral now, yaw rate max, a_max, stop distance, turning radius at speed);
- `StabilityMarginM` (SSM or DCM margin);
- per-limb phase, contact and load;
- `LimbCapability[]`;
- `State` (Walking | Braced | Falling | Fallen | GettingUp).

Attacks (bites, tail slams, shoves) are motor skills. They run as additional tasks in the same QP and are requested through a separate `MotorSkillRequest`, so locomotion and strikes share one force budget. That is out of scope here; the name is reserved.

### 8.2 Why intent and not torques
- Training torques for a 10,000 t, 60-DOF body per species (D-026, F-025) needs far more samples than training tactics, and must be retrained after every limb loss (F-024).
- The analytic controller keeps the M-001/M-002/M-006 guarantees.
- A policy cannot learn to "cheat" physics that it cannot touch. The physics stays honest (P1) and the policy stays tactical (D-005).

### 8.3 Sim-to-3D (offline prototype → Python 3D plant → Unity)
1. One telemetry format end to end. The Python plant writes `gait_log@v1` / `physics_log@v1`, so the same metric tools (M-001, M-002, M-006, M-130) score Python and Unity runs.
2. Bracket PhysX contact behaviour: two plant variants (§6.2.6). A controller is ready for Unity only if it passes both.
3. RL training runs on a **locomotion surrogate**:
   - envelope dynamics fitted to full-physics captures (first-order speed and yaw response, a_max, margins, a fall probability against margin);
   - periodic evaluation and fine-tuning in full physics;
   - the gap is measured: surrogate against full-physics envelope error ≤ 10 % [A].

   Training on full physics is possible later (Q-257).
4. Unity runs the same C# controller that dotnet tested on Python-recorded states (T-171), so the only new variable in Unity is PhysX.

### 8.4 Determinism constraints
- Fixed physics step. Controller and policy rates are expressed in **steps**, never seconds of wall time.
  - The Core clock default is 0.02 s (Assumed in CoreSettings), while R-005 recommends 1/60 s. The kaiju follows whatever the clock is, and its gains are derived per step.
- QP: double precision, fixed iteration cap, fixed constraint order, deterministic warm start (previous active set). No `Dictionary` enumeration in hot paths, no parallel reductions, no `Time.time`.
- Policy inference: CPU, single-thread, fixed op order; observation quantised to float32 before inference; decisions only at step indices `k mod 30 == phase`.
- Randomness (gait jitter, pack variation) only from seeded Core streams.
- Same-machine determinism only (PhysX, Enhanced Determinism on; M-006.8). Multi-body interaction is a known break (§6.2.7). The determinism test (T-175) runs 1, 2 and 3 kaiju and records where identity breaks, as the mech did.
