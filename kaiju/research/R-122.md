## 1. Question
What must the demo's HUD (D-019 limits: reactor power, heat, ammo, pilot strain), settings menu and accessibility basics look like so that they (a) stay inside the pixel look (P4, 5 palette profiles), (b) are usable by colour-blind, low-vision and photosensitive players, (c) are testable by an automated metric, and in what stages (task IDs) can they be built with the UI module still empty?

## 2. Headline
1. **Nothing of the HUD exists as a module.** `Assets/Game/UI` holds only `.gitkeep`. The only HUD is `SliceBootstrap.OnGUI` (one IMGUI label, top-left, feel-check text, not scalable, not themed). Of D-019 only **ammo** has runtime state (`Cannon.Ammo/MaxAmmo`, Combat); power, heat and strain have no state or contract yet (D-019 is `todo` in the ledger; Q-008 reactor figures unreconciled). So the HUD must be built on a read-only **limits view contract** with a scripted provider first (stage 1), real providers later.
2. **Colour alone cannot carry the gauges in this palette.** With a greedy search over each profile's own colours for 4 gauge fills (max-min dE under normal, protan, deutan, tritan simulation), `slate_teal` (17 colours, one hue family) reaches only dE 8.8 / dL 8.6 and `slate_crimson` has dL 2.7 between two of the four; fills against the darkest palette colour give **non-text contrast 2.3-3.1:1, below the 3:1 target** in all but lavender_peach (section 4.2). Hence: redundant coding (icon + label + fixed position + fill pattern) is mandatory and colour is only a third cue; this is also the standard accessible-design rule [3].
3. **Text size is solvable with an integer pixel font**: a 5x7 pixel font at k = 4 is 28 px body height at 1080p, above the 18 px PC minimum of XAG 101 [5]; 200 % scaling (the XAG upper bound) is k = 8 or an output-resolution fallback font. Non-integer text scale is forbidden by the look (Q-234 spirit).
4. **Rebinding needs a migration**: `DevicePilotSource` polls `Keyboard.current`/`Gamepad.current` directly (WASD, Space) rather than `InputAction`s, so Unity's rebinding API [6] cannot be used until the map is moved to an `InputActionAsset`. This touches Control (not mine); it is the one cross-module dependency of Track 4 and is staged separately (T-151).
5. **Photosensitivity is a measurable, cheap gate**: WCAG 2.3.1 [1] (<= 3 general or red flashes per second, or area <= 25 % of a 10 degree field) can be checked from frame dumps in Python on the existing capture clips, plus a settings switch that sets shake and flash gain to 0 (metric drafts M-NEW-d, M-NEW-e).

## 4. Findings

### 4.1 HUD architecture (recommended)
- **Contract** `Game.UI.Contracts`: `MechLimitsView` (read-only struct per mech per frame): `PowerFrac`, `PowerPeakFrac`, `HeatFrac`, `HeatStateEnum {Nominal, Warm, Overheat, Lockout}`, `AmmoCount`, `AmmoMax`, `StrainFrac`, `StrainStateEnum`, plus `SchemaVersion`. Providers: `ScriptedLimitsProvider` (for tests and capture), `CannonAmmoProvider` (real, from Combat), later Power/Heat/Strain providers when D-019 systems land (outside this track). The HUD never reads simulation internals.
- **Rendering**: a HUD render texture at the pixel pipeline's internal resolution (480x270 at 1080p, k = 4), palette-locked (every pixel an exact entry of the active profile, same invariant as A2), integer-upscaled by k together with the scene, so screenshots pass M-003 d-iv on the HUD too. Text uses a bitmap pixel font (5x7 base). Gauges: segmented bars (not smooth fills) so the quantisation is style-consistent.
- **Layout (reference 480x270)**: bottom-left mech block (power and heat as two 64x6 segmented bars with icon + 3-letter label), bottom-right weapon block (ammo count `18/20` plus pip row), strain as a vertical gauge beside the pilot portrait slot; top-centre only transient warnings (max 1 line). No element within the central 40 % of the frame (kaiju fights live there; P1 weight). Co-op: partner's compact block top-left (2-player demo, Q-230).
- **Warning language**: state changes flash at <= 2 Hz and only the *outline* (see 4.3), with an icon swap and an audio cue (XAG redundancy); thresholds Warm 0.6, Overheat 0.85, Lockout 1.0 (assumed, tuned with D-019 numbers).

### 4.2 Colour-blind-safe use of the 5 profiles (measured)
Method: the profile's distinct colours (decoded from `rgba`, R in the low byte); CVD simulation = Machado et al. 2009 severity-1.0 matrices [2] on linear RGB (matrices typed from the published tables; verify against the authors' page before relying on the third decimal); CIE76 dE in L*a*b* (D65). Candidates for gauge fills: L* > 35 and chroma >= 12. Panel colour = the darkest entry, text = the lightest.
| profile | colours | text on panel (WCAG contrast) | best 4 distinct fills: min dE over normal+3 CVD types | min dL* of the 4 | min fill vs panel contrast |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| lavender_peach | 29 | 19.0 | 23.6 | 14.9 | 3.10 |
| warm_amber_haze | 21 | 12.25 | 17.9 | 8.7 | 2.81 |
| warm_rust_sepia | 26 | 15.08 | 16.4 | 14.8 | 2.80 |
| slate_crimson | 29 | 7.0 | 11.3 | 2.7 | 2.67 |
| slate_teal | 17 | 7.86 | 8.8 | 8.6 | 2.32 |
Reading: text contrast is fine everywhere (>= 7:1, above the 4.5:1 AA body target). Four hue-coded gauges are **not** robustly separable in `slate_teal` or `slate_crimson`. Non-text elements (gauge fills) miss 3:1 against the darkest colour in 4 of 5 profiles. Remedies, in order: (1) 1-px light outline (the profile's lightest colour) around every gauge, which moves the contrast pair to outline vs panel (>= 7:1 all profiles) and costs nothing in palette; (2) gauge identity by icon, label, position, not hue; (3) state shown by lightness steps plus segment count (a gauge at 85 % has 2 fewer lit segments *and* a brighter outline), so Overheat is legible in greyscale; (4) never red/green pairs; for the heat state use the profile's most orange-or-red entry only as an additional cue [3]. A per-profile `hud_colours` table (chosen offline by this script, committed as data, sha-checked like the profile) avoids runtime search.
Caveat: simulation is an approximation [3]; the founders' colour-blind check is a real person's judgement and stays a tracked row (not a gate this research can close).

### 4.3 Photosensitivity: screenshake, flashes
- Standard: WCAG 2.3.1 [1]: no more than 3 general flashes (a pair of opposite relative-luminance changes >= 10 % of max, darker state below 0.80) or 3 red flashes per second, or flash area <= 0.006 sr within any 10 degree field (25 % of it). The Game Accessibility Guidelines state the same shape (more than 3 flashes/s over 25 % of the screen) at Basic level and warn against labelling a game "epilepsy safe" [7].
- Sources of risk in this game: building collapse white-out and muzzle/impact flashes (Combat, Destruction), quake camera shake (Seismic; the quake effect is a camera displacement, not luminance), HUD warning blink, lightning/explosion palette flashes. The pixel look uses few luminance steps (5-9 rungs per profile), so a single flash is often >= 10 % ΔL: every flash event must be treated as a general flash.
- Design: central `PresentationGain` (0..1) for shake and another for flash, both in Settings (default 1.0 for shake, flash capped by rule below even at 1.0); at 0 the shake offset passed to the Camera is exactly zero and flash overlays are skipped. Flash events carry a minimum spacing of 340 ms (<= 2.9 /s) regardless of gain, so the default build is under 3/s by construction; the metric verifies it.
- **Not** a medical certification: report the metric as "WCAG 2.3.1 automated screen", never claim safety.

### 4.4 Text scaling and readability
- Minimum default PC size 18 px body height at 1080p (36 px at 4K) and player scaling up to 200 % without loss of function [5]. Pixel-font ladder at 1080p: S = 5x7 at k=4 (28 px), M = 7x10 font at k=4 (40 px), L = 5x7 at k=8 (56 px, 200 % of S). 4K: k = 6 -> 42 px base; ladder multiplies by integers only. Layouts reflow (no horizontal+vertical scrolling at L); long strings wrap at <= 40 glyphs per line at L.
- Text on busy scenery gets a 1-px outline in the profile's darkest colour (XAG shows outlined in-world text as an accepted pattern [5]).
- Subtitles (plans/M3.md Track 4): same font ladder, semi-opaque panel, speaker label; any audio-only information (low-power warning, kaiju roar direction) gets a text/icon equivalent.
