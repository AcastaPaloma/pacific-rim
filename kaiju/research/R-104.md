## 1. Headline

- One currency, USD. A Balanced mech costs about **$551M** (9,000 t, $61k/t); a Heavy Brawler about **$563M** (11,500 t, $49k/t); the city-funded Basic Balanced floor chassis about **$331M** (8,300 t), free, not resellable.
- Price = sum over part classes of mass x installed price per tonne. The class prices sit between heavy-equipment ($10k/t, Cat 797F [8]) and fully integrated warship ($130k-250k/t [1][2][3]); steel and titanium inputs come from market indices [4][5][7].
- Contract payout = fee fraction x value saved, with value = sum of sourced replacement costs of the protected assets [9][10][11][12][13]. Fee fraction 0.06 **(A)**. A typical contract (protected value about $1.2B, 75% saved) pays **about $55M** (p10-p90 $25M-$117M), capped at 35% of a Balanced price.
- Repair costs 1.5 x (1 - integrity) x part price, capped at replacement. Typical sortie damage costs $10M-$30M. Ammo and reactor upkeep 0.4% of the mech price per sortie.
- Salvage: a wreck is recovered by mass as (1 - fragmentation) x location efficiency. A toppled mech's specific fall energy is gh = 392 J/kg, about 4% of a 10 kJ/kg comminution scale, so wrecks stay in large pieces: **0.86 by mass on land, 0.60 in the shallow Bay, 0.30 in the deep channel**. Credit value is 5-12% of the build price (reused parts at 60% of price x integrity, scrap at 0.3 of material price).
- Kaiju tissue is a material class: bone (compressive strength 153-205 MPa [16]) gives osteoderm plate with about 1.5x the specific strength of armour steel, so equal-strength armour is about 36% lighter. A heavy kaiju (about 57,000 t) yields about 340 t of usable bone. Rare parts are unlocks, not currency.
- Pacing (draft, section 8): median **12 sorties** (p10-p90 8-19) to afford a full replacement after a loss while flying the floor chassis; about 3.4 mech losses per 80-sortie campaign; loss-farming earns less than a tenth of honest play.

| Asset | Replacement cost | Source and caveat |
|---|---|---|

Buildings use footprint x floors x $6,000/m2 from World.Geo/Destruction data; the three landmark rows above override. Airport and rail rows are TODO (Q-NEW-c).

Loss ratios per damage state reuse the Hazus-style scale already in R-021 section 1 (`DamageState` None/Slight/Moderate/Extensive/Complete = 0/2/10/50/100% of replacement cost; the ratios are the Hazus structural defaults, to be cross-checked against R-021 when T-118 starts).

Payout for contract k with asset set A_k (building IDs and fixed assets):

- V_k = sum over assets of replacement cost.
- L_k = sum over assets of replacement cost x loss ratio(state at contract end), read from `BuildingDamaged` events (published once per state change [Destruction contracts]).
- saved S_k = (V_k - L_k) / V_k.
- payout = min( f_fee x (V_k - L_k) , cap ) with f_fee = 0.06 **(A)**, cap = 0.35 x Balanced price ($193M). Failure floor: if S_k < 0.25 the payout is 0 (contract failed), not negative.
- Upfront advance 10% of the expected payout at accept, repaid from the payout (so a mech can be repaired before a sortie); no debt, the ledger never goes negative.

Typical numbers: V = $1.2B median (lognormal, sigma 0.6, so $0.5B-$2.6B for p10-p90), S = 0.75, payout $55M median. The Bay Bridge as a single protected asset would pay up to $390M and is capped at $193M; that is deliberate (a landmark defence should feel decisive, and the cap keeps one sortie from replacing a mech).

Why paying on saved value: it is the only term the physics decides (what survived), it rewards protecting rather than killing, and it makes kaiju infrastructure targeting (F-044) cost the player directly.

## 4. Repair cost per damage state

Part state from integrity fraction I = Integrity/MaxIntegrity (the fields `PartDamage` already carries; thresholds follow `PlaceholderDamageRecord` 2/3 and 1/3):

| State | I | Repair cost (share of part price) | Time (hangar steps) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intact | >= 0.95 | 0 | 0 |
| Light | 0.66-0.95 | 1.5 x (1 - I), at most 0.5 | 1 |
| Moderate | 0.33-0.66 | 1.5 x (1 - I), at most 0.9 | 2 |
| Heavy | 0-0.33 | 1.5 x (1 - I), at most 1.0 (replace) | 3 |
| Destroyed | 0 | 1.0 (replace) minus the recovered part credit | 4 |

Rationale: repair re-welds and re-plates the missing fraction of material plus access, inspection and re-qualification labour. The factor 1.5 stands for that overhead **(A)**; the cap at 1.0 means a part is never repaired for more than it costs to replace. Mass changes when a part is lost, replaced or repaired, so repair never silently changes the CoM: the refit validator runs on exit from the hangar. One hangar visit may queue as many parts as funds allow; unfunded repairs stay queued (`Hangar` already carries queue state). Damage carries over exactly (D-017): the repair step processes only what the player pays for.

Per sortie, typical repair cost on a Balanced mech is $10M-$30M (about 2-5% of its price); sortie upkeep (ammo, reactor fuel) is 0.4% of price ($2.2M).

## 5. Salvage physics

### 5.1 Fragmentation and recovered mass

A part of mass m that absorbs energy E fragments by a first-order law, with specific energy e = E/m:

- f_frag = 1 - exp(-e / e_c), e_c = 10 kJ/kg **(A)**, taken as the low-to-mid scale of comminution (Bond work indices of 1-10 kWh/t = 3.6-36 kJ/kg; the choice is a design constant, not a measured property of a mech).
- Toppling from CoM height 40 m: e = g h = 392 J/kg, so f_frag = 3.8% for a part that absorbed the whole fall; concentrated weapon hits give e of 10-50 kJ/kg locally and fragment those parts. Result: a fall barely fragments, a focused kill does.
- Recovered mass m_rec = m (1 - f_frag) x eta_loc, with eta_loc = 0.90 land, 0.70 shallow Bay (wading depth), 0.35 deep channel (about 100 m deep: diving and heavy lift needed) **(A)**. The 0.90 is bounded by ship breaking, where about 95% of a hull is mild steel and sold by lightweight tonnage [14], and by demolition, where about 76% of C&D debris is recovered (secondary source) and steel recycling is reported near 98% (industry claim) [15]; the game discounts both for a wreck lying in a dense city.

For a 9,000 t wreck: land 0.96 x 0.90 = **0.86**, shallow 0.67, deep 0.34. The three numbers in the headline round these.

### 5.2 Value of recovered mass

Per recovered part: reuse value = reuse_factor x I x part price, reuse_factor 0.6 **(A)** (inspection, re-qualification, no warranty), only for a part with I > 0.33; otherwise scrap value = scrap_factor x raw material price, with scrap_factor steel 0.3 **(A)**, titanium 0.1 (from [5] $7.70/kg over $80/kg), composite 0.05 **(A)**. Reactor and weapons parts are reuse-only (cannot be scrapped for cash, they are removed under city control).

Worked example: a 9,000 t Balanced wreck in which 40% of the cost-weighted parts survive at I about 0.7 on land: 551M x 0.4 x 0.7 x 0.6 x 0.86 x 0.9 mix = about $60M, which is 11% of the build price. Across the loss distribution the toy simulation uses a credit of 10% x U(0.3, 1.0) of the price. Design rule: credit is capped at 12% of the build price, so losing a mech always costs at least 88%.

### 5.3 Building debris

`BuildingDamaged.DebrisKg` (increment, kg) is the input. Steel fraction by building type from the Destruction catalogue; recovered by the same eta_loc. Debris recovery is paid as scrap only (steel $980/t x 0.3 = $290/t), a bonus line on the contract, small by design (a 100,000 t collapse pays about $29M x steel share). It is deliberately not a farming route (section 7).

### 5.4 Kaiju tissue as a material class

A kaiju carcass enters salvage as a mass budget by tissue class. Mass uses the section 4 volume law with an animal density rho = 1,050 kg/m3 **(A)** (not the 1,000-2,500 band for hollow frames): V = 0.07 (l/1.8)^3 m3.

| Species (D-026) | Height | Volume | Mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| light | 60 m | 2,590 m3 | about 2,700 t |
| mid | 100 m | 12,000 m3 | about 12,600 t |
| heavy | 165 m | 53,900 m3 | about 56,600 t |

| Tissue class | Mass share **(A)** | Property used | Rare part it unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| bone (osteoderm, cortex) | 0.12 | compressive strength 153-205 MPa [16], density about 1,900 kg/m3 (to verify) | osteoderm plate: specific strength 170/1,900 = 89 kN m/kg vs HSLA 450/7,850 = 57, so 1.56x, equal-strength armour mass x 0.64 |
| hide and keratin laminate | 0.06 | tough, low density | liner: absorbs spall; mass x 0.8 in the armour liner slot (properties **(A)**, no source this pass) |
| tendon and muscle fibre | 0.35 | contractile fibre | actuator fibre bundle: torque density bonus in a limb slot (no sourced figure; keep capped at +15%) **(A)** |

Recovery is throughput-limited, not mass-limited: the salvage fleet moves a fixed tonnage per game hour before decay and the next emergence, so usable recovery of a class is min(class mass x eta_loc, throughput x window). Throughput 80 t/h x 48 h window **(A)** = 3,840 t shared. For a heavy kaiju this gives bone 6,800 t x 0.9 but throughput-limited: about 340 t of bone after prioritisation (5% of bone mass; the rest decays or is left). Value at specific-strength parity with titanium: 0.45 x $120k/t = $54k/t, so 340 t is about $18M: a minor cash bonus, a major catalogue unlock. Rare parts require a minimum recovered mass per part (osteoderm plate set for one chassis arm: 25 t) and are consumed on fitting; they are not currency, not tradeable and not collectible (Q-205).

Tissue is damaged by how the kaiju died: tissue class mass is reduced by the same f_frag law applied to the damage energy the kaiju body recorded (the `IBodyDamageRecord` shape is reused: the kaiju damage record exposes per-part integrity, `Meta.Salvage` reads it as `SalvageInput`).

## 6. Permadeath and the anti-dead-end floor

Rules (all in `Meta.Roster`):

1. A mech instance is **Lost** when, at fight end (`EnterHangar`), its core part (cockpit-torso block or reactor plant) has Integrity = 0, or the player scraps it. Destroyed limbs, weapons, armour are not a loss: they are repaired or replaced.
2. Lost is absorbing: no state transition leaves it (D-020). The roster keeps a tombstone (record stays, for the pilot record, the wreck id and the event log).
3. A wreck is produced once, with a stable `WreckId` (deterministic from mech id and step); the salvage handoff is idempotent by `WreckId`, so replaying the event or a save reload cannot salvage twice.
4. The pilot survives the mech with a strain penalty: pilot strain from F-008 is recorded; a mech loss adds strain recovery of 1 sortie (the pilot flies the floor chassis at full strain 0.5 after a loss). No pilot death in v1 (Q-NEW-d: recommend defer).
5. Each slot (co-op N slots, D-004) has one pilot record; the treasury is shared per campaign.
6. **Floor (Q-206).** If a slot has no flyable mech after a loss, the city funds a Basic Balanced chassis ($331M, 8,300 t, structure+armour+drive+reactor+light weapon, no titanium or composites). Free, flyable next hangar cycle, non-resellable, cannot be refitted beyond the basic loadout catalogue, and not granted while the slot owns any flyable mech (cannot be stacked). It is granted regardless of funds, so there is no state with no flyable mech.
7. Ledger invariant: funds >= 0 always (unfunded purchases and repairs are queued, not debited); upkeep is paid down to 0 and the remainder is carried as a flag, never as debt.
8. Floor chassis is weaker by design: 0.7 effectiveness in the toy model (smaller mass, fewer weapons), which makes replacement purchases the player's goal without making a loss fatal.

Why the floor is a physical statement, not a handout: the city keeps a reserve hull (one of a standing series) and the player gets it, but its limbs and weapon slots are the catalogue's lowest tier; every upgrade still has to be earned.

## 7. Exploit analysis (no unrecoverable state, no exploitable loop)

Unrecoverable states: none reachable, because (a) the floor chassis is granted on loss, (b) the ledger cannot go negative, (c) any contract can be declined (no obligation debt), (d) a flyable mech and at least one open contract always exist (contract generator guarantees one contract with payout >= 25M and loss risk below the floor chassis's capability).

Loops (each must lose money):

| Loop | Guard | Net |
|---|---|---|
| lose the mech on purpose for wreck credit | credit <= 12% of price; floor chassis non-resellable | loses >= 88% of price per loss |
| buy a part, sell it back | sell-back 40% of price | loses 60% |
| repair then scrap | scrap 0.3 raw, reuse 0.6 | loses |
| contract-farm a trivial asset | payout on saved value of that asset; small V pays small | linear, no multiplier |
| destroy a building to collect scrap | scrap $290/t steel vs cost of ammo and repair per sortie | tested in M-104 |
| fail a contract then retry for the advance | advance repaid from payout; failed contract forfeits the advance | net 0 |

Co-op: shared treasury but the floor chassis is per slot; one player's loss does not drain the other's repair queue (per-slot repair queues exist already in `hangar@v1`).

## 8. Pacing targets and the toy simulation

Targets (draft, to be tuned in T-123): sortie 15-25 min; hangar visit after every sortie; mech loss about once per 20-30 sorties; replacement affordable (full price from net income, floor chassis flown meanwhile) in **8-16 sorties median**; a campaign is 60-80 sorties (about 25 h); first refit unlock (a weapon swap) after 3 contracts; first rare part after the first heavy kaiju kill.

Toy simulation (Python, 30 seeds x 80 sorties, log-normal contract value, beta-distributed saved fraction and damage, loss probability 0.03 + 0.12 d^3 x difficulty, surplus above 0.25 x price spent on refit, floor at 0.7 effectiveness, f_fee 0.04/0.05/0.06/0.08):

| f_fee | median sorties to replace | p10-p90 | losses per campaign | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.04 | 28 | 17-41 | 3.7 | too slow |
| 0.05 | 16 | 11-23 | 3.5 | band edge |
| **0.06** | **12** | **8-19** | **3.4** | chosen |
| 0.08 | 9 | 5-12 | 3.1 | fast, loss feels cheap |

Loss-farming control (lose every sortie): ends with about $0.3B vs. $1B+ for honest play over the same 80 sorties on the same seeds; the control never reaches a full replacement without the floor. The model is simplified (no repair queueing, no refit upgrades, independent sorties); T-123 replaces it.

## 9. Refit constraints from physics

One modular frame (D-025): 13 slots: head/sensors, torso core, 2 shoulders, 2 upper arms and forearms (arm slots), 2 hip-leg slots, 2 feet, back mount, reactor bay. A part has mass, centre-of-mass offset, power demand, heat, torque rating and cost. Two chassis classes are constraint sets over the same slot list.

Limits per chassis (derived numbers; all values are data in `parts@v1`/`chassis@v1`):

| Constraint | Formula | Balanced | Heavy Brawler |
|---|---|---|---|
| total mass | M <= p_g A_foot / g, p_g = 700 kPa static ground pressure **(A)** (soils bear 100-300 kPa; the mech is allowed to sink, see section 4 of the breakdown at 650 kPa) | A = 140 m2, cap 9,900 t | A = 190 m2, cap 13,000 t |
| CoM height | 0.45 l-0.55 l, l = 80 m | 36-44 m | 36-44 m |
| CoM lateral | within 1.5 m of the sagittal plane | same | same |
| CoM fore-aft | within [-2, +3] m of the ankle midpoint | same | same |
| static tilt margin (capsize) | atan(stance half-width / h_cm) >= 12 deg after losing one limb | half-width 12 m: 16.7 deg | 15 m: 20.6 deg |
| reactor power | P_rated = 0.2 MW/t x reactor mass; sum of peak demands <= P_rated + buffer 1 GJ over 20 s | 180 MW | 260 MW |
| heat | sustained: Q_gen <= Q_rej = 30 MW passive + 0.2 MW/t x radiator mass **(A)**; burst: stored heat C_th x dT <= limit, 2.1 GJ/K per 500 t coolant, dT 60 K | 600 s burst profile | 600 s burst profile |
| actuator torque, per slot | tau_rated = 2,000 N m/kg x actuator mass x 0.8 derate **(A)** >= tau_demand | see below | see below |

Passive heat figure: surface about 3,950 m2 (human 2 m2 scaled by (80/1.8)^2); radiating at 600 K and emissivity 0.9 gives 26 MW, convection at 60 K excess with h = 25 W/m2K gives 6 MW: so about 30 MW passive, and a 100 MW load needs coolant plus radiators.

Torque demand per slot: tau = m_limb g r_cm + I alpha + payload term. Shoulder (arm 400 t, 35 m, alpha = 1 rad/s2 consistent with Froude timing t proportional to sqrt(l)): holding horizontal 68.6 MN m, accelerating I = m L^2 / 3 = 1.6e8 kg m2 gives 160 MN m, an 80 t weapon at 35 m adds 27.5 MN m static plus 98 MN m dynamic: total about 355 MN m, so about 222 t of actuator mass per shoulder. Hip about 300 MN m for balance, knee about 400 MN m. The sum of actuators in a Balanced mech is about 1,500 t, consistent with the class table. Brawler alpha = 1.2 rad/s2.

Reactor load check: walking power = cost of transport x m g v: with COT 0.34 (human walking, metabolic **(A)**), m = 9,000 t, v = 2.5 m/s gives about 75 MW; a thruster burst of unweighting 20% of the weight about 100 MW for at most 20 s; a heavy arm cannon pulse 30 MW. Peak demand of a Balanced loadout (walk + one cannon + one burst) = 205 MW, above 180 MW: the buffer (1 GJ) covers the 25 MW excess for 40 s, which bounds how long the burst can run. This is the intended refit tradeoff (D-019): the validator rejects builds whose peak minus rated exceeds buffer/20 s.

Validation: ordered checks (slot types, mass cap, power, heat, torque, CoM box, tilt margin), each returning the first violated constraint with its numeric margin. A valid build produces `MechBuildSpec@v1` (section 10). Mass, CoM and inertia tensor are computed from parts by the parallel-axis theorem so T-005 can read them directly.

## 10. Contract proposals (engine-free, deterministic)

Common: no `UnityEngine`, no `System.Random` (use Core's seeded RNG contract), money as `long` whole US dollars in the ledger and Float64 bits only for physical quantities (mass, CoM, integrity) so `hangar@v1`'s exact-bit pattern is reused. Flags `meta.economy`, `meta.salvage`, `meta.permadeath`, `meta.refit`, default off (plans/M2.md Track 4).

### 10.1 `Meta.Economy` (`economy@v1`)
- Service `IEconomyService`: `Funds` (long), `OfferContracts(seed)`, `Accept(ContractId, PlayerSlot)`, `Complete(ContractId, ContractOutcome)` (computes payout per section 3), `TryPurchase(price, reason)`, `Credit(amount, reason)`. Invariant funds >= 0.
- Types: `ContractId` (long counter), `Contract {Id, State, AssetIds[], Value, Advance, Payout, AcceptedBy}`, `PriceQuote {PartId, UnitPrice, Mass, Source}`.
- Save: section `economy`, version 1. Records `treasury` (funds, next_contract_id, seed), `contract:<id>` (state, value_usd, advance, payout, slot, step), `tx:<n>` (amount, reason code, step; ring of last 256). Everything int64 except ratios stored as Float64 bits.
- Reads sourced prices through `IPartCatalog` (below) and replacement costs through `IAssetValueProvider` (adapter over Geo/Destruction in the composition layer, same pattern as `IBodyDamageRecord`).

### 10.2 `Meta.Salvage` (`salvage@v1`)
- `ISalvageService`: `Assess(SalvageInput) -> SalvageResult`; `Claim(WreckId)` idempotent; `Inventory` of recovered parts and rare materials by class (mass, Float64 bits).
- `SalvageInput {WreckId, PartDamage[] parts, MassByPart, Location (Land/ShallowBay/DeepChannel), SourceKind (Mech/Kaiju/Building)}`.
- Save: section `salvage`, version 1: `wreck:<id>` (claimed, location, recovered_kg), `inv:<class>` (mass_kg Float64 bits), `rare:<partId>` (count, min_mass).
- Physics constants (e_c, eta_loc, reuse and scrap factors) live in a `salvage_params@v1` data table, each with source or Assumed flag.

### 10.3 `Meta.Roster` (`roster@v1`)
- `IRosterService`: `Mechs(slot)`, `Create(chassis, spec)`, `Retire`, `OnFightEnd(IBodyDamageRecord)` (evaluates the loss rule, publishes `MechLost` once), `GrantFloor(slot)`.
- `MechInstance {MechId (EntityId), Chassis, State {Flyable, Lost}, SpecHash, WreckId, Cycles, Pilot}`; `Pilot {Slot, Strain Float64, Sorties}`. Lost is absorbing; transition table checked in tests.
- Save: section `roster`, version 1: `mech:<id>` (chassis, state, spec_hash, wreck_id, cycles), `pilot:slot:<n>`, `floor:slot:<n>` (granted_count).

### 10.4 `Meta.Refit` (`refit@v1`, catalogue `parts@v1`, output `MechBuildSpec@v1`)
- `IPartCatalog`: parts with `{PartId, Class, Slot, MassKg, ComOffset(x,y,z), PowerPeakW, HeatW, TorqueNm, PriceUsd, Rare, Source, Assumed}`; `chassis@v1` holds the slot list and limits of section 9.
- `IRefitService`: `Validate(chassis, assignment) -> RefitReport {Ok, FirstViolation, Margins}`, `Apply(MechId, assignment)` (hangar phase only, pays through economy, publishes `RefitApplied`), `Build(...)`.
- `MechBuildSpec@v1`: chassis, per-slot part ids and masses, total mass, CoM (x,y,z), inertia tensor (6 floats), power (rated, peak), heat (rejection, storage), joint torque limits per slot, hash. All float64, deterministic; the physics body reads it once T-005 lands (flag `pendingT005Body` stays on until then; the placeholder body ignores it).
- Save: section `refit`, version 1: `mech:<id>/slot:<name>` (part_id, wear Float64 bits). Catalogues are not saved (data), only assignments.

### 10.5 Core bus events (unmanaged, IDs only)
```
struct ContractCompleted : IEvent { long ContractId; PlayerSlot Slot; byte Outcome; long PayoutUsd; }
struct MechLost          : IEvent { EntityId Mech; PlayerSlot Slot; long WreckId; byte Cause; }
struct SalvageRecovered  : IEvent { long WreckId; byte SourceKind; byte Location; double RecoveredKg; long CreditUsd; }

```

### 10.6 Wiring

## 11. Metric drafts

### M-104 economy pacing, no unrecoverable state, no exploitable loop
- Definition: three sub-gates over a deterministic simulation of the real modules, 30 seeded campaigns x 80 sorties, scripted contract mix and loss model from `salvage_params@v1`.
- Procedure: T-123 simulator drives `Economy`, `Roster`, `Salvage`, `Refit` through the public interfaces; seeds 1-30; record per loss the sorties until a full replacement is purchased; run the loss-farming and sell-back controls on the same seeds.
- Thresholds: (a) median sorties to replace within 8-16, p90 <= 25; (b) 0 states with no flyable mech, 0 negative funds, 0 unreachable contracts over all steps; (c) every guard loop of section 7 returns net funds below the honest baseline by at least 60% of price per iteration.
- Source: this spec (bands from section 8, a toy model; thresholds are not tuned to pass, escalate rather than adjust).

### M-105 refit physical consistency
- Definition: every valid build satisfies the section 9 constraints; every invalid build is rejected with the correct first violation.
- Procedure: enumerate all chassis x slot assignments reachable from the catalogue under a seeded random generator (10,000 per chassis) plus the hand-picked edge builds; recompute mass, CoM, power, heat, torque independently in the test.
- Thresholds: 0 valid builds out of limits (mass <= cap, CoM in box, tilt margin >= 12 deg, torque ratios >= 1, peak power minus rated <= buffer/20 s); 0 invalid builds accepted; spec totals equal the sum of part masses to 1e-9 relative; same seed gives the same build hash.
- Source: section 9.

### M-106 save round trips
- Definition: all four sections (`economy@v1`, `salvage@v1`, `roster@v1`, `refit@v1`) restore bit-exactly across 3 cycles (save, restart, load), as M-059.
- Procedure: as `Hangar.Tests/M059Tests`: 3 seeds x 3 cycles, a file save and a simulated process restart at every phase change, floats compared as raw bits; plus a flipped-bit control.
- Threshold: 0 differences over all comparisons; the flipped bit is counted; corrupted sections give `SaveLoadStatus.Corrupt` and the services stay untouched (validation before mutation, as `hangar@v1`).
- Source: `Assets/Game/Hangar/MODULE.md` M-059 pattern.
