## Headline
- **Both bind, at different speeds.** The **controller binds now**: a flat-footed stance with a force-only stance leg is kinematically capped at **Fr 0.005–0.025** (step 0.23–0.38 l for stance knee 25°–5°), which is exactly where T-005 lives (stable at 0.25 l, slides at 0.27–0.31 l, falls at ≥ 0.33 l). The **body binds near Fr 0.20**, not by geometry but by margins: friction on the stance foot, and the waist that must cancel the legs' yaw momentum.
- **True feasible range with the body as given:** Fr **≤ 0.20 with 5° joint margin** (≤ 0.28 at zero margin) for a gait with human-like foot rockers and 10° pelvic tilt; **≤ 0.11** with R-007's narrower rocker bounds. At every speed above Fr ≈ 0.01 the stance foot cannot supply the yaw moment alone: the trunk must cancel **≥ 71 % (Fr 0.10) and ≥ 81 % (Fr 0.20)** of the limbs' vertical angular momentum, (at the 0.67-utilisation shares 0.80 / 0.87 the waist turns 21–23° of its ±30° and uses 54–76 % of its 5e8 N·m force limit).
- **R-007 vs the reference:** the implementer's numbers are the right ones. R-007 was optimistic on both points: it checked joints only at the double-support instant, and it modelled the yaw reaction with the thigh alone.
- **Recommendation:** merge the infrastructure now, flags off. Make one cycle on stance-leg impedance around a corrected reference with trunk momentum compensation, expected **Fr 0.10–0.15**, with M0 accepted at the registered band floor of **Fr ≥ 0.10**. Then use offline trajectory optimisation plus a whole-body QP for **Fr 0.17–0.20**. A learned tracking policy is the fallback. No assist.

## 3. Why the controller binds, and the architectures that close the gap

**Why a force-loop stance sits in a narrow basin** (derived from R-005 §5.2.6 and the evidence):
1. **No posture term.** Jacobian-transpose virtual forces map a 2-D CoM force plus a trunk torque onto 3 sagittal stance joints. The map is square, so nothing regulates the leg's shape. The knee settles wherever the force balance leaves it: 10–20° in T-005, which the flat-foot cap (0.30 l at 15°) matches.
2. **The CoP clamp forbids rollover.** The ankle torque is clamped so the CoP stays 10 % inside the sole. Heel rise needs the CoP on the toe line, so by construction the foot stays flat and the step is capped kinematically (§1: Fr ≤ 0.025).
3. **Past the cap, the ankle limit takes over.** Beyond the cap the ankle reaches its −20° stop. The PhysX limit constraint, not the drive, then sets the ankle torque, the CoP jumps to the toe edge, and the yaw budget halves (0.85e8) while the demand rises (≥ 2e8 at 0.3 l). The foot twists: slides of 1–3 m at caps 0.27–0.31, falls at ≥ 0.33.
4. **Pieces added one at a time contradict each other.** Tracking the whole reference fails because the stance leg does not follow the reference's CoM path while the swing and upper body do. Only the upper-body momentum terms are self-consistent, and those are the ones that helped (Hu5).
5. **Engine inverse dynamics needs the contact forces.** Unity's `GetJointForcesForAcceleration` "does not consider gravity" and ignores drives [2]. A floating root adds 6 Jacobian columns [3]. Contact forces are absent, and no mass-matrix call is listed in 6000.6 [1]. Joint-space feed-forward for a stance leg therefore needs contact forces from a model: that is a whole-body controller's job.

## 6. Rulings requested by the implementer (proposals)

**C20 multi-mech independence.**
- **Exact independence is not achievable.** Enhanced determinism guarantees an unchanged simulation when added actors "do not interfere with the existing actors" [6]. Two articulations stepping on one shared static ground interact through the shared contact and solver bookkeeping, even though no force passes between them.
- The evidence shows the following:
  - bit-identical results against inert, dropped and standing bodies;
  - a first divergence only while the other mech changes its contact set (step 462, 0.9 mm at 10 s);
  - growth without bound afterwards (1.9 m at 20 s in cycle 3).
  The coupling is therefore a float-level perturbation amplified by a gait whose Lyapunov growth exceeds 10×/10 s. No physical tolerance on the 20 s CoM is meaningful.
- **Proposed criterion (M-NEW-b):**
  1. Bit-identical when the others are non-walking (passes today).
  2. With walking others, the first divergent step's CoM difference ≤ 1e-4 m, about 3 float32 ulps at 300 m (ulp 3.05e-5 m).
  3. The 20 s divergence lies within the 95th percentile of a self-perturbation ensemble: A alone with a 1e-6 m root perturbation, 10 runs.
  4. No shared state in module code (already shown).
- The two diagnostic runs (separate ground colliders; speculative contacts off) stay useful to name the cause, but they are not gating.

**M-006.5 limp-fall energy account.**
- The engine reports KE and PE exactly, and drive work through `GetDriveForces` × joint velocity.
- It does not report joint-limit constraint work: no such query is in the 6000.6 list [1].
- `ContactPairPoint.impulse` is documented only as "the impulse applied to this contact pair" [7], so its friction component is unverified.
- Closure against ΔPE with drive work only cannot reach ±5 % (0.913 measured; 8.7 % is unlogged friction and limit work).
- **Proposed `M-006@v1.1` .5 limp:**
  1. **Passivity:** the sum of per-step gains of (KE + PE + cumulative drive dissipation) is ≤ 1 % of ΔPE, and no single step exceeds the `M-006.7` 7.8e7 J.
  2. **Released energy to rest** equals ΔPE to rest, 3.6 GJ ± 10 % (as in the locked case).
  3. **Residual** R = ΔPE − KE − W_drive is reported, not gated. It is split into an estimated friction work μ_d Σ J_n |v_t| from contact points (point velocity of the foot link) and the remainder, attributed to limits.
