## 0. Headline and assumptions
### 0.1 Headline
1. **Three layers, three clocks.** Strategic (pack goal: hunt vs raid, 1 Hz, minutes-long decisions), operational (roles, slots, board, 1 Hz plus events), tactical (`IKaijuBrain`, 5 Hz, unchanged). Bay distances (13-47 km between targets, 20-100 min of walking, §3) make the strategic layer a different problem from R-024's 4 km field; the tactical brain only ever sees the last ~1.2 km.
2. **Four roles, decentralised auction.** Anchor, Flanker, Bait, Raider (+ `Free` while unassigned). Each member bids from its own state using a species-fit table; a consensus auction (CBAA-style [4]) over delayed pack messages resolves conflicts. N <= 5, so the allocation itself is trivially cheap; what matters is that it only uses information the member could have.
3. **No omniscience by construction.** The "board" is not a global blackboard. Each member owns a private copy; entries enter only from the member's own senses or from a pack call that was physically deliverable (range, latency distance/343 m/s, loss). Static infrastructure positions are *learned map knowledge* (allowed); infrastructure *condition* and mech positions are not.
4. **Raid vs hunt is an optimal-foraging problem.** Rate-maximising choice over travel time plus handling time (marginal value theorem [5]); raids double as bait because mechs defend infrastructure.
5. **M-007@v2** keeps every v1 clause and threshold (C1-C6, no threshold lowered), adds 3 groups (role P1-P6, infrastructure I1-I4, formation F1), five new controls (§5.3) and a "cheating" positive control so the panel instrument can fail.
6. **Contracts untouched.** Everything is implemented as a wrapper `PackMemberBrain : IKaijuBrain` over any inner brain plus a pack-owned optional `IDirectedBrain` interface that the T-041 policy can implement later (§7).

## 1. Question
How should 2-5 kaiju of up to three species behave as a pack across a 1:1 Bay so that a human sees intelligence (not cheating), a metric can measure it, an RL policy can later be trained into it (F-022, D-015) and it fits the M-008 budget?

## 2. Findings: what the sources say, and what we take
### 2.1 Pack hunting in animals (design inputs, not thresholds)
- Wolf pack tracking, pursuit and encirclement emerge from two local rules without signalling or hierarchy; pack of 4-5 surrounds best (R-024 [R24-1]). Our *tactical* layer stays decentralised for this reason.
- Lion hunts: stable wing/centre positions, higher success when animals take preferred positions (Stander 1992, R-024 [R24-2]). Role preference by individual is legitimate; our species-fit table is the same idea.
- A review across Carnivora finds group hunts vary in organisation, no evidence that cooperative hunting needs advanced cognition, and that aggression-reducing social mechanisms and *information transfer between individuals* help [2]. Take: roles can be simple and rule-based, but a pack needs a cheap information channel (the call) and a way not to fight itself (hysteresis, §4.3).
- Group size effects on success are non-linear in wolves (MacNulty et al. 2012, "Nonlinear effects of group size on the success of wolves hunting elk", Behav Ecol) (cited from memory, *not re-fetched*; used only to justify the cap of 5 and "a pair is already a pack").
- Fission-fusion: carnivore groups split and merge by resource and risk (spotted hyena, Smith et al. 2008, *not re-fetched*). We use it for "a pack may split a Raider off" and "two small packs may merge when in call range", capped at 5.
### 2.2 Role allocation (robotics and MARL)
- Multi-robot task allocation is, for instantaneous single-task single-robot problems, an optimal assignment problem; time-extended and multi-robot tasks are harder (ST-SR-IA vs MT/MR/TA taxonomy) [3]. With N <= 5 members and <= 5 slots we solve ST-SR-IA exactly (Hungarian or brute force 5! = 120) *if the data were global*; decentralisation then only adds the consensus.
- CBAA/CBBA: market-style bidding plus consensus over local communication produces conflict-free assignments that tolerate **inconsistent situational awareness and changing network topology** [4]. That is exactly our constraint (members have different, delayed boards), so §4 adopts a CBAA-style single-assignment auction.
- Learned roles: ROMA conditions policies on a stochastic role embedding; roles are dynamic and identifiable [6]. Take: give the policy an explicit role/directive input (pack layer assigns, policy executes) rather than hoping roles emerge; roles stay inspectable for the metrics (P1-P6).
- Team reward mixing: OpenAI Five trained five-agent teams with PPO at very large scale [7]; the "team spirit" individual/team reward blend is from memory of the same paper (*not verified here*). Take: F-022's pack terms should be mixed with individual terms and annealed, §8.
### 2.3 Game AI, "smart, not cheap"
- Halo 2: a behaviour DAG with squad-level behaviours; the design aim is that the player believes they face a creature with intentions [8]. Our strategic/operational/tactical split follows it.
- Alien: Isolation: a macro "Director" that *is* omniscient only suggests broad areas and pacing; the alien itself must find the player with its own senses; a menace gauge backs the alien off when pressure is high (secondary summaries of Thompson 2017) [9]. This is the pattern we want for the strategic layer: it may *suggest* where mechs probably are (from calls and last-seen), it never hands out a position. Pressure/pacing backing off is a feature, not a flaw (Retreat, raid-as-bait).
- F.E.A.R.: small state machine plus planner; flanking claim must be verified geometrically (R-024 [R24-3]); retained in every flank clause.

## 3. The Bay scale problem (distances, speeds, trade-off)
### 3.1 Distances and walking times
Coordinates are *Assumed to +/- 1 km* (from memory of well-known sites; Track 1 T-105 replaces them with baked positions). Straight-line (haversine), real routes are 1.2-1.5x longer around deep water. Time at cruise 7.9 / 9.12 / 11.2 m/s:

|---|---|---|

| Hayward fault (Oakland) - Port of Oakland | 7.5 | 16 / 14 / 11 |
| Golden Gate Br. - Port of Oakland | 13.6 | 29 / 25 / 20 |
| Port of Oakland - OAK airport | 13.1 | 28 / 24 / 19 |
| SFO - OAK | 17.7 | 37 / 32 / 26 |
| Port of Oakland - SFO | 21.1 | 45 / 39 / 31 |
| Golden Gate Br. - SFO | 24.1 | 51 / 44 / 36 |

Consequences: (a) **transit dominates beyond ~5 km** (a 5-minute fight vs a 25-minute walk); (b) all three species are within a factor 1.4 in speed, so a *mixed* pack can move together at the slowest member's pace without a large penalty (move at v_pack = 0.9 min(v_i), assumption), but a single L cannot outrun a mech (7.9 < 9.12), while M/H can; (c) mechs (9.12 m/s) cover 13 km in 24 minutes: a defending mech team can reach a raid on the *same* side of the Bay but not across a deep channel unless bridges/ferries exist (route cost field, Track 1/2); (d) time budget per session matters (open question Q-NEW-b).
### 3.2 Water and ground
Kaiju wade (F-045). Depth/H rule *Assumed*: passable if depth <= 0.4 H, so L (24 m) cannot cross the Golden Gate channel (tens of metres deep; USGS bathymetry via Track 1), M (40 m) and H (64 m) still avoid it. The Bay is shallow elsewhere, so the main water barrier is the Golden Gate/Raccoon Strait/Carquinez channels; the pack's route planner uses a cost field with depth/H and soft-ground terms (§6.1). Raid paths therefore go through bridges' approaches (which are also targets) or around the south Bay.
### 3.3 Hunt vs raid utility (optimal foraging)
Patches are clusters of F-044 targets (Port of Oakland cranes, the Golden Gate/Bay Bridge pair, the airports, substations); a mobile patch is the mech team. Marginal value theorem: leave a patch when its instantaneous rate falls below the pack's long-run average rate including travel [5] (empirical support is mixed, so this is a design rule, not a claim about animals). For goal j with value V_j, success probability P_j (from board: mech strength estimate, defenders seen), travel time T_j = path cost / v_pack, handling time h_j (structure resilience / pack damage rate):
`U_j = (V_j P_j - R_j) / (T_j + h_j)`, R_j = risk term = expected loss value (mass-weighted kaiju hp at risk x P(defended)).
- Hunt goal: V = mech disabled value (hp-weighted), position known only from board (age-discounted, §4.1).
- Raid goal: V = class weight x destructible fraction (Assumed weights: crane 1, power node 2, bridge span 3, airport 3; per-target hp from T-105 Destruction); known static positions, *unknown condition* until visited.
- Pack goal is re-evaluated at 1 Hz with hysteresis: switch only if U_new > 1.3 U_current and >= 30 s dwell (Assumed) to avoid wandering between continents.
- **Raid as bait ("feint").** Defended infrastructure draws mechs (they have to respond, D-015 is the pack's goal, mechs' goal is defence). A pack with >= 3 members and a Bait may raid with a subset while hunters hold a flank position on the mech approach line. This is the F-022 "baiting" term in a form measurable at Bay scale (P4, I4).
- **Split rule:** a pack of >= 4 with two raidable patches within 8 km may detach one Raider (heavy preferred) for <= 10 min; it must rejoin or be re-merged by call (fission-fusion). A pack of <= 3 never splits (hunt group needs Anchor + one more).

## 4. Pack model
### 4.1 Communication model: what a member may know
Channels (all *Assumed* numbers, config `pack_comms.json`; ground truth cited where noted):
| Channel | Content | Range / delay / loss | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Own sight | targets in LOS and R_s (species), occluded by terrain/buildings once `World` provides it | per §0.2; harness: none, `visible` flag still logged (R-024 §1.2) | R-024 |
| Own hearing | footfalls/weapon noise of mech at <= 2.5 km, bearing + coarse distance only (no id, +/- 20 deg, +/- 25 % range) | 2.5 km, delay d/343 s | *Assumed* |
| Pack call (low-frequency "rumble") | board delta: observer id, entity id, position, heading, hp class, **timestamp**, role claim, bid | range 5 km air (3x species H/60 scaling), delay d/343 s (5 km = 14.6 s), 10 % loss/hop, 1 Hz max per sender, payload <= 8 entries | elephants respond to calls at ~2 km, detect ~4 km, modelled infrasound/seismic cues to 6-16 km, soil/noise dependent [10]; wolf howls carry 10-16 km open [11] |
| Relay | a member within range of two others forwards the latest deltas once | +1 hop delay | *Assumed* |
| Static map knowledge | positions of F-044 targets and landmarks (not condition) | unlimited; tagged `known_static` | design: animals know territory |
Rules: (1) a target entry has `observer`, `time_observed`, `source` {own, heard, called, relayed, map}; the member uses **age-extrapolated** state (last position + velocity x age capped at 20 s, then cone of uncertainty) never the live truth. (2) Condition (hp, destroyed or not) of a remote entity is the last *observed* value; "death is public" (R-024 rev 3) applies only within the member's own sensing. (3) A member far outside call range simply has no board updates; it keeps the last plan, then falls back to `Free` and a solo goal after `board_staleness_s` = 60 s. (4) **Leak definition** for C4: any decision input (target id, position, ally role, infrastructure condition) with no provenance entry in the member's log inside its allowed latency window.
Rationale: a 160 m kaiju should plausibly *shout* across the Bay; making the shout slow and lossy makes pack coordination visibly imperfect at long range and crisp up close, which is what viewers read as intelligence (Alien: Isolation backs the macro AI's omniscience out of the creature's own decisions [9]).
### 4.2 Roles
| Role | Intent (what the pack layer asks of the member) | Species fit f(species, role), 0-1, *Assumed* |
|---|---|---|
| **Anchor** | engage the main threat at standoff, hold position, soak fire, break cover/structures between | L 0.1, M 0.5, H 1.0 |
| **Flanker** | take a slot at +/-70 deg from the pack-to-target bearing (R-024 1.4), commit when spread >= 90 deg | L 0.9, M 0.8, H 0.2 |
| **Bait** | be the nearest/most visible threat at the edge of enemy weapon range, kite tangentially, never enter melee; retreat early (hp < 0.6) | L 1.0, M 0.4, H 0.0 |
| **Raider** | transit to and destroy an F-044 target; fights only in self-defence | L 0.3, M 0.7, H 1.0 |
| `Free` | no assignment yet (new, lost contact, board stale) | n/a |
Required roles by pack size n and goal: hunt n=2 {Anchor or Flanker, Flanker or Bait}; n=3 {Anchor, Flanker, Flanker or Bait}; n>=4 {Anchor, Flanker, Bait, +any}; raid n>=1 {Raider, +Anchor escort for n>=3}. Pack of five hunts with 1 Anchor, 2 Flankers, 1 Bait, 1 flex (Anchor/Flanker by auction).
### 4.3 Allocation and re-allocation (decentralised)
- **Bid.** member i for slot s: `b_i,s = f(species_i, role_s) * (0.5 + 0.5 hp_i) * pos_i,s * (1 - switch_cost_i,s)` with `pos` = path-time-based fit (a flanker already on the correct side bids higher; a heavy farther than 3 km from a raid target bids lower), `switch_cost` = 0.25 if the member currently holds another role and has dwelled < 20 s, else 0.
- **Consensus.** CBAA-style rounds [4]: each member broadcasts its winning-bid vector `(slot, bidder, bid, stamp)`; a member drops a slot if it hears a higher bid (ties: lower EntityId ordinal wins, determinism rule in Core). Converges in <= n rounds in the theory; here rounds ride on 1 Hz calls so convergence time <= n s plus delay (<= 20 s at 5 km). Inconsistent boards are tolerated by design [4].
- **Triggers for re-allocation:** member death or Retreat (state 4/5), contact loss > 10 s, target dead, goal change, a role slot uncovered > 5 s, pack join or split. **Hysteresis:** minimum dwell 20 s in a role, bid margin 1.15 to take over, at most 1 voluntary role change per member per 20 s. Forced changes (death, retreat) are exempt from the thrash count (P2) but counted in P3.
- **Covering rule on loss.** If the Anchor dies, the best remaining fit by auction becomes Anchor; if no member fits (fit < 0.3) the pack changes goal to Retreat/Regroup (withdraw to the nearest ally), not suicide (R-024 Retreat rule; hp < 0.35).
### 4.4 Shared target board
Per member private store `Board` (capacity 16: 8 targets, 8 infrastructure entries within 25 km, plus ally states <= 7): `BoardEntry {id, kind {mech, infra, ally}, pos, vel, hpClass, conditionKnown, observer, tObserved, source, claimedBy}`. `claimedBy` is the role/focus claim (a "soft lock": the focus target is the one with most claims and best score; a claim older than 6 s expires). Focus fire (C2) is produced by claims plus R-024's focus bonus, not by a master. Board to tactical brain: `BrainObservation.Targets` is refilled from the board *filtered to age <= 3 s memory window for combat* (R-024 fairness) and allies with `Role` filled. Board to strategic layer: the full aged board.
### 4.5 Emergence from faults, then pack formation (F-020)
1. A World.Seismic rupture selects an emergence site along a fault segment (T-114); k = 1-5 kaiju emerge in a spatial/temporal cluster: positions drawn within 0-4 km along the rupture, times within 0-120 s *Assumed*. Species mix chosen by the director from a table keyed by magnitude (larger event, larger species), seed-driven.
2. **Formation rule.** Co-emerged members within call range auto-form a pack if the composition is valid (size 2-5, at least one species with fit >= 0.6 for Anchor/Flanker/Raider). Singletons call at emergence and wait up to `join_wait_s` = 180 s; any pack within call range with size < 5 may admit them (fission-fusion). Members that hear no reply start as solo raiders/hunters and may be adopted later.
3. Formation latency target: F1 below.
4. **Seismic damage precondition.** The same rupture liquefies susceptible fill near the site (§9), so infrastructure can start the scenario damaged. Raid value V_j uses the *observed* condition, so a pack does not know an unvisited crane is already down.

## 5. M-007@v2 (full spec)
M-007 text unchanged (verbatim above). v2 changes: scale (Bay), species (mixed packs), pack layer, infrastructure, controls, scoring. **No v1 threshold is lowered.** All thresholds are *Assumed* design targets pending the founder calibration sitting (T-125), as in v1.
### 5.1 Definitions (additions to R-024 §3)
- **Engaged(T)**, **flanked**, `FR_commit`, `FF`, `FE`, voluntary switch, leak: as R-024 §3.1-3.4 with D_eng scaled per species: D_eng = 5 H_target_species where the *kaiju's own* H sets the distance (L 300 m, M 500 m, H 800 m); spread >= 90 deg unchanged. (Rev 3 addendum: FE vs independent baseline, thrash window 12 s.)
- **Role time** of member i: fraction of its alive engaged ticks with role r in its decision rows (new `role` values and `board_age_s`, §6.4).
- **Bait-and-strike event:** a window [t, t+30 s] in which (a) a kaiju B holding role Bait is the target (by the mech's own fire/aim, harness field `mech_target`) of >= 1 mech for >= 3 s, and (b) another pack member F reaches Charge on that mech from a bearing >= 90 deg away from the mech->B bearing within the window. **Opportunity:** a window with (a). **BS rate = events / opportunities.**
- **Kill-exchange ratio** KX = (sum of mech hp removed / total mech hp) / (sum of kaiju hp lost / total pack hp), per run; pooled as ratio of sums.
- **Re-allocation latency** L_r: seconds from a loss event (kaiju death or Retreat entry) to the first tick at which all required roles for the surviving pack size (§4.2) are claimed by distinct live members.
- **Straggler fraction** SF: share of pack-goal time in which a member is > 2 x call range from its nearest packmate while the pack goal is hunt or raid-with-escort.
- **Infra value destroyed** I_val = sum over destroyed F-044 targets of class weight x destroyed fraction (weights §3.3). **Raid completion:** >= 1 target of the pack's committed raid goal destroyed >= 80 %.
- **Abandonment fraction** AB: share of ticks, with any mech within 1 km of >= 1 pack member, in which >= 50 % of the pack are in transit (Raid, away from the mech) AND the pack goal is hunt. **Idle fraction** ID: share of pack-goal time with no mech within 5 km where no member makes progress (distance to goal falls < 0.5 m/s mean over 60 s) and no member fights.
- **Pack outcome score** S = (mech hp removed / total mech hp) + 0.5 (I_val / I_val,max) - 0.5 (kaiju mass-weighted loss fraction), range [-0.5, 1.5]. Weights *Assumed*, mirror F-022 terms; fixed before any policy trains, never tuned to a result.

### 5.5 Human panel clause (C5 v2)
>= 12 raters, each watches 8 blinded clips of 60-90 s with a scripted free camera (not a player view): 2 clips each from `scripted pack (candidate)`, `solo@1`, `omniscient@1`, `directcharge@1`; at least one clip per family (hunt, raid, mixed). 7-point items: "smart", "cheap", "coordinated", "fair" (did it know things it should not), free text "what looked cheap?". Pass: median smart >= 5 and median cheap <= 3 for the candidate **and** paired Wilcoxon p < 0.05 that `omniscient@1` is rated cheaper than the candidate (instrument validity: if raters cannot tell the cheat, the panel result is void) **and** candidate smart > `solo@1` smart (p < 0.05). ### 5.6 Win-rate clause (C6) in detail
v1's "sides swapped" is meaningless for kaiju vs mechs. v2 defines it as a **paired-seed contest**: the same seed fixes spawn, mech behaviour seeds and map; pack A (policy) and pack B (scripted baseline) are each run once; the outcome score S (5.1) is compared. Win rate = proportion of seeds with S_A > S_B (ties count half). Threshold unchanged (>= 0.55, Wilson lower bound > 0.50). ## 7. `Game.AI.Pack` design (new assembly, T-113)
- **Layer / deps:** L3, pure C# (`noEngineReferences`), references **only** `Game.AI.Contracts` (+ `Game.Core.Contracts` transitively). Never references `Game.AI.Scripted`, `Game.AI.Harness` or `Policy`; never edits them (M2 boundary 14).
- **Contract gap and solution.** `BrainObservation`/`BrainIntent` have no field for role, goal or infrastructure target. Instead of editing Contracts, Pack defines (a) its own `PackDirective {Role, Goal, FocusTargetId, SlotPos, RoutePos, AllowMelee}` and (b) an optional `IDirectedBrain : IKaijuBrain { void Decide(in BrainObservation obs, in PackDirective d, ref BrainIntent intent) }` that the T-041 policy adapter may implement (recommendation to the M2 lead and T-041's owner: expose role and directive as additive observation inputs in the encoder; no Contracts change needed).
- **Types:** `PackMemberBrain : IKaijuBrain` (wraps an inner brain; when inner is not directed, applies observation filtering + intent post-processing per role: Bait clamps the approach band at 0.9 x weapon range and kites tangentially, Anchor speed 0.5, Raider overrides to Transit/Raid intents); `PackBrainFactory : IKaijuBrainFactory` (BrainId `pack@1`, flag `kaiju.packBrain`, default **off**, in `Config/FeatureFlags/AI.json`); `PackCoordinator` (per pack, ticked by the host at 1 Hz; owns goals and the auction, but reads only members' *own* board copies via messages); `Board`, `BoardEntry`, `CommsModel` (range/latency/loss from `pack_comms.json`, seeded); `RoleAllocator` (CBAA rounds); `GoalSelector` (U_j + hysteresis); `IInfrastructureSource` (positions, class, value, hp, condition; adapter in Composition over `IInfrastructureRegistry`, because Pack may not reference World); `IRoutePlanner` (path time and next waypoint; straight-line and grid A* provided; Bay field adapter later); `ISpeciesLookup` (species spec numbers from `Units.Kaiju`, via Composition); `PackLog` events for `brain_log@v2`.

## 8. RL implications (requirements on T-041 / T-116; R-028 absent)
- **Hierarchy:** the policy is the tactical layer only; the pack layer assigns role/goal/route. Rewards for the policy stay F-022 but are split: individual terms (damage dealt to mechs, infrastructure destroyed, penalty for damage taken) plus pack terms (flank, bait, focus) credited by the role it was assigned, so a Bait is not penalised for taking damage that the role requires (credit by role, reviewed in T-116).
- **Anti-gaming (Goodhart):** pack-coordination shaping terms must not be the M-007 measure values themselves; shaping uses potential-based forms (angular spread progress, focus claim agreement) while evaluation uses held-out seeds, unseen compositions and the panel. A policy is accepted only on held-out compositions (train on 6 of 9, test on 3) and on `omniscient@1`-free leak checks.
- **Observation fairness for training:** the training environment must feed the same board/provenance the runtime does (no leak-by-simulator). C4 runs on training rollouts too.
- **Species conditioning:** one policy per species (F-025) with the species' scalars and the role/directive as inputs; the mixed pack is the unit of evaluation.
- **Staging (tracked, not cut):** scripted pack layer first (T-113, T-115); policies slot into `IDirectedBrain` when T-041's architecture merges (T-116).

## 9. Liquefaction and tsunami (M2 research items)
**Liquefaction.** Source data exist: USGS susceptibility mapping of Quaternary deposits for the central Bay region (OFR 2006-1037, supersedes 00-444 for the core; Bay mud rated *moderate*, artificial fill rated by the unit beneath, five classes) [12]. In the 1989 Loma Prieta event liquefaction concentrated on fill (Marina District, Port of Oakland, SFO) (well-known; not fetched, treat as unverified for the report). **Does it change fights? Yes, in two ways, both cheap to model:** (1) *Infrastructure state at start*: a seismic emergence event liquefies susceptible fill, so port cranes, runways and fill-side power nodes begin damaged (raids on them have lower remaining value; the `conditionKnown` rule of §4.1 makes unvisited targets a real information problem). (2) *Mobility*: Bay mud is soft, undrained strength typically < 25 kPa [13], giving a strip-footing bearing capacity of ~5.14 x 25 ~ 130 kPa (own arithmetic; textbook formula, not checked against the source). A graviportal heavy with contact pressure above this sinks on mud flats and fill; R-102's foot pressure decides. Recommendation: a per-cell `softness` factor from the susceptibility class in the route cost field (§6.1), used by `IRoutePlanner` and by `IInfrastructureSource.Condition`. No new rendering. Owner: Track 1 (data fetch) + T-113 (cost field), spare R-105 for an R-022 follow-up on ground pressures if R-102 flags a problem. Decided autonomously (founder asleep): **in M2, traversability and start-condition only; not dynamic lateral spreading.**
**Tsunami.** The SAFRR scenario (M9.1 Alaska peninsula source) is the standard Bay tsunami reference and shows port and low-lying damage [14] (report chapters not read; used only as the source catalogue). The Bay's active faults are mostly strike-slip, which generate small tsunamis, and emergence is tied to those faults (F-020), so a tsunami would not accompany an emergence. Kaiju wading produces ship-wake-scale waves (F-045), not tsunami. **Does it change fights? No at gameplay scale; at most an external event for scenario variety.** Recommendation: **not in the M2 gate**; implement wake/splash from F-045; keep a tsunami/seiche as an M3 optional environmental event (tracked row), driven by the SAFRR inundation maps if the founders want it. Decided autonomously (founder asleep).
