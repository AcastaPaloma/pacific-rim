## 1. Froude band (steady walking)

**Relations.**
- Fr = v²/(g l), so v = √(Fr g l) [2].
- Relative stride length λ/l = 2.3 Fr^0.3 (Alexander's mammal fit [1]).
- Stride period T = λ/v = 2.3 Fr^-0.2 √(l/g). Step period = T/2. Cadence = 120/T steps/min.
- Duty factor β = stance time / T. Double-support fraction of the stride = 2β − 1.

**Validation on a human** (l = 0.93 m, Fr 0.20): 1.35 m/s, stride 1.32 m, T = 0.98 s, 123 steps/min. This matches normal human walking (preferred Fr ≈ 0.25 [3]).

**Reference mech table** (l = 42.4 m):

| Fr | v (m/s) | v (km/h) | stride λ (m) | step (m) | T stride (s) | cadence (steps/min) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 (band floor) | 6.45 | 23.2 | 48.9 | 24.4 | 7.58 | 15.8 |
| 0.15 | 7.90 | 28.4 | 55.2 | 27.6 | 6.99 | 17.2 |
| **0.20 (nominal cruise)** | **9.12** | **32.8** | **60.2** | **30.1** | **6.60** | **18.2** |
| 0.25 (human preferred) | 10.20 | 36.7 | 64.3 | 32.2 | 6.31 | 19.0 |
| 0.30 (band ceiling) | 11.17 | 40.2 | 68.0 | 34.0 | 6.08 | 19.7 |
| 0.50 (walk–run transition, forbidden) | 14.42 | 51.9 | 79.2 | 39.6 | 5.49 | 21.8 |

Height range at Fr 0.20: 76 m gives 8.89 m/s with T = 6.43 s; 88 m gives 9.57 m/s with T = 6.92 s.

**Band: Fr ∈ [0.10, 0.30], nominal cruise Fr = 0.20.** The nominal value is *Assumed, pending calibration*.
- **Floor 0.10.** This is the lowest end of typical elephant walking (Fr 0.10–0.24 [6]). A human at Fr 0.10 walks at 0.96 m/s, below the net-cost-optimal ≈ 1.05 m/s [22], which reads as dawdling. Viewers infer body size from stride frequency through the pendulum law, f ∝ 1/√l [8]. Timing slower than any natural cruising gait for this leg length therefore stops reading as "bigger" and starts reading as slowed footage. This is the same failure as overcranking a miniature beyond the √scale frame-rate rule [9]: "slow-motion / underwater".
- **Ceiling 0.30.** This is above human preferred walking (0.25 [3]). Larger species choose lower relative speeds (preferred Fr falls with body size [7]). Approaching the walk–run transition at Fr ≈ 0.5 [2] the gait shortens double support and looks hurried and athletic, which reads as light. Above 0.35 the gait is a defect. Fr ≥ 0.5 is physically a run: the inverted pendulum loses contact near Fr = 1, and elephants become aerial at Fr ≈ 1 [5].
- **Nominal 0.20** sits between graviportal (0.10–0.24 [6]) and human (0.25 [3]) values. The leg-geometry stilt result (optimum Fr ≈ 0.17 [3]) also supports a value below 0.25 for long, heavy limbs.
- **Duty factor β ∈ [0.60, 0.72]**, so double support is 20–44 % of the stride. Human walking: stance ≈ 60 %, double support ≈ 20 % (two periods of ≈ 10 %) [10]. Elephants keep high duty factors across speeds, and larger individuals have higher β [5]. A heavy walker should therefore sit at β ≥ 0.60. The ceiling of 0.72 (DS 44 %) is *Assumed*: above it the gait reads as shuffling. At Fr 0.20 the stance lasts 3.96–4.75 s, which is 198–238 physics steps at 50 Hz.
- **Consistency check:** the measured stride period must be within ±15 % of T(Fr) above. Alexander's fit is a mammal-wide regression, accurate to roughly this scatter [1]. A mech whose speed is in band but whose legs cycle at the wrong rate (skating stride) fails.

Confidence: high for the formulas and the 0.5 transition [1][2]; medium for the band ends (literature plus a perceptual argument; founders calibrate).

## 2. Peak acceleration and deceleration limits (centre of mass)

Under dynamic similarity, a/g is invariant with size: accelerations scale as l/t² ∝ l/l. A giant moving "like a human" would therefore show human-sized accelerations in m/s², with all times stretched 6.75×. Heaviness comes from the time stretch plus the physical ceilings below. Each ceiling is taken at the reference mech.

| Limit | Formula | Value (m/s²) | Value (g) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Traction (friction) | μ g, μ = 0.4 | 3.92 | 0.40 | μ *Assumed*. For sand, φ ≈ 30–35° and δ/φ < 1 give tan δ ≈ 0.35–0.55 [25]. Ground under 650 kPa feet yields (§4), so use the low end. Pending a soil R-task (Q-NEW-e). |
| Tipping, single support, forward (CoP at toe) | g·(0.75 L_f)/h | 3.22 | 0.33 | ZMP/LIPM criterion: a = g (x_zmp − x_com)/h, with ZMP inside the support polygon [19]. |
| Tipping, single support, backward (CoP at heel) | g·(0.25 L_f)/h | 1.07 | 0.11 | Braking in single support is weak, so stopping needs a step. |
| Tipping, single support, lateral | g·(W_f/2)/h | 0.84 | 0.085 | Sets the lateral and curve limits (§3). |
| Tipping, double support, fore–aft (step 30.1 m) | g·(s/2 + 0.75 L_f)/h | 6.51 | 0.66 | Not binding. |
| Power at cruise | (P − c m g v)/(m v) | 1.70 | 0.17 | P = 200 MW *Assumed* (20 W/kg: a tank is ≈ 25 hp/t ≈ 18.6 W/kg [24]; a 100,000 t carrier is ≈ 190 MW shaft [23]). c = 0.05 is the mechanical cost of transport: net metabolic ≈ 2 J/(kg·m) [22], i.e. ≈ 0.20 dimensionless, × an assumed 25 % muscle efficiency. Walking itself draws 44.7 MW at Fr 0.20. Power stops binding below ≈ 6 m/s (2.8 m/s² at 6 m/s). |
| Structural / large-animal | — | — | — | Peak tissue stress grows with size unless posture changes. This caps the manoeuvrability and acceleration of large animals [18]. It supports staying below human-like relative loads; it does not give a number. |

**Limits adopted for M-001** (CoM horizontal acceleration, smoothed as defined in §9):
- **Instantaneous fore–aft: |a_x| ≤ 0.30 g = 2.94 m/s².** This is 75 % of the traction limit and below single-support forward tipping (3.22). Human overground walking already reaches about 0.3–0.4 BW of fore–aft ground force [17] (low confidence), so a heavy walker should stay at or below the human relative level, never above it.
- **Instantaneous lateral: |a_y| ≤ 0.085 g = 0.84 m/s²** (single-support lateral tipping).
- **Net (stride-averaged) acceleration from a start: ā ≤ 1.38 m/s² (0.14 g).** This means reaching Fr 0.20 cruise takes no less than 2 steps (§4). It is also below the power limit (1.70).
- **Net deceleration on a normal stop: ā ≤ 1.84 m/s² (0.19 g)**, i.e. at least 1.5 steps. On an emergency stop, the instantaneous 0.30 g ceiling applies, giving a minimum stop of 3.1 s over 14.1 m.
- **Mass dependence** at fixed height (fixed power, P/m changes): the physical minimum time from 0 to 90 % of 9.12 m/s is 2.79 s at 6,000 t, 2.99 s at 10,000 t and 3.88 s at 15,000 t. At cruise the power limit is 3.16, 1.70 and 0.97 m/s² respectively. At 15,000 t, power binds below the 1.38 m/s² feel limit.

Pilot strain (F-008) is out of scope here. The cockpit sits at ≈ 0.93 H, so yaw and pitch accelerations add tangential acceleration there; the pilot-strain R-task should reuse these limits.

## 3. Turn-rate limit

**Traction ceiling (not binding).**
- Turning in place (feet side by side, centres ±0.06 H = ±4.8 m): yaw torque ≤ μ m g d + 2 μ (m g/2) r̄, where r̄ = 5.53 m is the mean distance of the sole from its centroid. That is T ≤ 4.05×10⁸ N·m, giving α = T/I_z = 0.85 rad/s² (48.8 °/s²). A 90° bang-bang turn would take 2.7 s.
- In double support while walking (lever = step/2 = 15 m): T ≤ 5.9×10⁸ N·m, α ≤ 71 °/s².
- Traction is ≈ 12–18× above the gait limit below, so it never decides the turn rate.

**Gait / dynamic-similarity limit (binding).**
- Healthy young adults turning 180° while walking: peak yaw rate 181.6 ± 30.0 °/s, duration 1.98 ± 0.30 s, mean ≈ 91 °/s [16].
- Angular rate scales as √(g/l): ω_mech = ω_h √(l_h/l) = ω_h × 0.148. Angular acceleration scales as g/l: × 0.0219.
- **Peak yaw rate |ψ̇| ≤ 26.7 °/s** (scaled human peak).
- **Mean yaw rate on a 180° turn ≈ 13.5 °/s**, so a 180° turn takes **≥ 13.4 s**.
- **Yaw acceleration |ψ̈| ≤ 4.0 °/s²** (human ≈ 182 °/s², estimated as peak rate / half the turn duration, × 0.0219).

**Curve walking.**
- The turn rate is ω ≤ a_lat,max / v.
- With a_lat,max = 0.085 g (single-support lateral tipping, §2), ω ≤ **5.3 °/s at cruise (9.12 m/s)**. With 0.10 g, ω ≤ 6.2 °/s. The tighter value is adopted: stepping outward permits more, but reads as lurching. *Assumed, pending calibration.*

**Perceptual sanity check.**
- At 26.7 °/s the shoulder tips (r ≈ 13.6 m) move at 6.3 m/s, comparable to cruise speed. Faster turns would make the upper body outrun the legs and read as light.
- A 90° heading change in ≥ 6.7 s, at about 2 steps per 90°, matches how large animals pivot: through foot placement, never by spinning on a planted foot.
- The analysis tool treats yaw rate < 5 °/s as "not turning". The human 15 °/s turn-detection threshold [30] scales to 2.2 °/s (×0.148); it is raised to 5 °/s to sit above solver yaw noise (*Assumed*).

## 4. Time-to-start and time-to-stop (from Fr 0.20 cruise, 9.12 m/s)

Time is expressed in steps (step = T/2 = 3.30 s) and in pendulum units t* = t/√(l/g), so the result transfers to other sizes. Humans reach steady gait within 1–4 steps depending on the criterion; one-step attainment of steady velocity is reported [20]. Planned stops require extra braking impulse and a final double stance [21].

| Event | Band (steps) | Band (s) at reference | t* band | Distance | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Start, 0 → 90 % of cruise | **2–4** | **6.6–13.2** | 3.2–6.3 | 30–60 m | The human 1–4 step range [20], shifted to the heavy end for P1. The ceiling of 4 steps is *Assumed, pending calibration* (responsiveness). The physical minimum is 3.0 s (§2). |
| Normal stop, cruise → < 0.3 m/s | **1.5–3** | **4.9–9.9** | 2.4–4.8 | 23–45 m | Human planned stops are 1–2 steps [21]; heavy end for P1. The ceiling is *Assumed*. |
| Emergency stop (minimum) | ≥ 0.94 | ≥ 3.1 | ≥ 1.49 | ≥ 14 m | v/(0.30 g). Anything faster is unphysical. |

**"Matches mass".** Two conditions must hold:
1. t_stop ≥ m v / F_brake,max, with F_brake,max = min(0.30 m g, F_act,max from config).
2. Across mech variants (76–88 m, 6,000–15,000 t), the stop t* must stay within the band above, and must rise with m when actuator or power limits bind (see the §2 mass table).

## 5. M-002 foot-slip definitions

- **Foot length L_f:** the sole length along the foot's forward axis, from the header. Reference: **19.6 m**.
- **Threshold:** 0.005 L_f = **0.098 m** (9.8 cm). For a human-proportioned 11.5 m foot it would be 5.8 cm.
- **Contact (one contact c):** a maximal time interval in which foot i's normal force F_n ≥ F_on = 0.03 m g, ended when F_n < F_off = 0.01 m g (hysteresis).
  - These thresholds scale force-plate practice to body weight. Gait events usually use a 20 N threshold (5–20 N range) [28], i.e. ≈ 3 % of a 700 N body weight. Toe-off detection is most accurate at ≤ 10 N (≈ 1.4 % BW) [28].
  - Reference values: F_on = 2.94 MN, F_off = 0.98 MN.
  - Contacts shorter than 0.1 T_nom are tagged `scuff` and reported separately. They are not exempt from the threshold.
- **Sole points:** five points P_k in the foot frame, given in the header: the sole centre and the four corners at (±0.45 L_f, ±0.45 W_f) on the sole plane.
- **Planted (point level):** within contact c, point k is loaded at sample t if its distance along the contact normal above the lowest sole point is ≤ h_tol = 0.01 L_f (0.196 m).
  - Heel-to-toe roll therefore unloads the heel without counting as slip: tilting 0.01 rad about the toe moves the heel 0.196 m up but only 2 mm horizontally.
  - Measuring heights relative to the lowest sole point makes sinkage (650 kPa > soil bearing, §4) count as sinking, not slip.
- **Slip per contact:**
  s_c = max over k, over loaded samples t, of ‖Π(p_k(t) − p_k(t_k⁰))‖.
  - t_k⁰ is the first loaded sample of point k in c. The anchor is kept even if the point unloads and reloads within c, so drift accumulates.
  - Π projects onto the tangent plane of n̄_c, the force-weighted mean contact normal of c (d − (d·n̄)n̄).
- **Exclusion:** contacts with an external (non-ground) force on the body > 0.05 m g at any sample (combat shoves, grapples) are excluded. Sliding under an overwhelming push is physically true (P2). Locomotion captures contain no external forces, so nothing is excluded in them.
- **Sampling needed:**
  - Log **every fixed physics step**, with no decimation and fixed Δt ≤ 0.02 s (≥ 50 Hz). The simulated body changes state only at physics steps, so per-step logging is exact (no aliasing).
  - A stance of 4 s yields ≥ 200 samples. A slip of 0.098 m over the stance is 2.5 cm/s, below any velocity-based detector; this is why the metric uses displacement, not the velocity thresholds of motion-generation skate metrics [27].
  - Positions must be logged in float64 world coordinates (floating origin added back, F-042). float32 spacing is 1 mm at 10 km and 7.8 mm at 100 km, i.e. up to 8 % of the threshold.
- **Footskate as a defect class:** feet moving when they should be planted [26]. Under physics (F-001) slip comes from contact solver drift, PD fighting the IK target, or traction saturation. The metric is the same for all three causes.

## 6. Footfall-to-camera coupling (F-009), as a measurable definition

- **Contact event:** a `contact_begin` event fires at physics step n₀, the first step with F_n ≥ F_on (§5).
- **Camera impulse onset:** the physics step n_cam at which the camera rig receives the impulse, and the first render frame f_cam whose camera pose includes it.
- **Latency:**
  - L_step = n_cam − n₀. **Threshold: L_step = 0 in ≥ 99 % of contacts, ≤ 1 in 100 %.** F-009 says "at the exact contact frame".
  - L_frame = f_cam − f₀, where f₀ is the first render frame whose interpolated physics time ≥ t(n₀). **Threshold: L_frame ≤ 1.**
  - One frame at 60 fps is 16.7 ms, inside the audio–video detectability window of +45 / −125 ms [29]. That window also bounds the footstep audio onset (same rule, measured on the audio event timestamp).
- **Vertical impact momentum:**
  J_c = m · max(0, v_z,com(t₀ + τ) − v_z,com(t₀)), with τ = 0.10 T_nom.
  - τ is the dynamically scaled loading-response window (0–10 % of the gait cycle [10]). Reference: 0.66 s = 33 steps at 50 Hz.
  - Pendulum estimate at cruise: CoM descent speed at heel strike ≈ v sin(20.8°) = 3.2 m/s, so J ≲ 3×10⁷ N·s.
- **Amplitude:** A_c is the logged peak camera translational offset of the impulse (m).
  - **Threshold: every contact yields exactly one impulse (no misses, no duplicates).**
  - A log–log fit A = k J^γ must give **Pearson r ≥ 0.90** and **γ ∈ [0.5, 1.0]**, both *Assumed, pending calibration*. Perceived vibration magnitude grows compressively, so γ < 1 is allowed.
  - Capture set: walks at Fr 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, a normal stop, an emergency stop and a stomp (spread of J ≥ 3×).

This is proposed as a separate metric, **M-NEW-a**.

## 7. Panel rubric: "feels like 10,000 t" (part of M-001)

**Material.**
- Frame strips: 10 frames per stride (one every 0.1 T) over 3 strides, plus the real-time clip. These come from three cameras:
  - (a) the gameplay third-person rig;
  - (b) a ground-level side view at 500 m with human-scale props (cars, people) in frame;
  - (c) an aerial three-quarter view.
- Scenarios: cruise walk, start, normal stop, 90° turn in place, curve walk.
- Builds are anonymised. Each evaluator scores each scenario 1–10, then gives an overall score.

| Score | What the evaluator sees |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Toy or puppet. Human-rate cadence (> 60 steps/min at 80 m), instant starts and stops, feet skate, no ground response. |
| 3–4 | A person in a suit. Plausible pose but timing too fast (Fr > 0.4 look), body turns faster than the feet can be placed, camera static at footfalls. |
| 5–6 | Big but wrong. Either floaty / slow-motion (overstretched timing, Fr < 0.1 look, drifting CoM, no weight transfer) or correct timing with weightless contacts (no dust, no sink, no impulse). |
| 7 | Heavy most of the time. Correct cadence and visible weight transfer, but one clear tell: a visible foot slip, a start or stop under 2 steps, a snap turn, or off-sync camera or dust. |
| **8 (pass)** | Consistently heavy. CoM leads into each step and is caught by the landing foot. The stance foot is pinned and sinks. Dust, debris and camera impulse land on the contact frame and scale with stomp strength. Starts take ≥ 2 steps and stops ≥ 1.5 steps with visible lean. Turns are made by foot placement. No tell in any scenario. |
| 9 | As 8, plus secondary motion sells the mass: armour and limbs settle after each footfall, the upper body lags in turns, and ground deformation persists in the footprint trail. |
| 10 | Indistinguishable from a physically real 10,000 t walker. An evaluator cannot name a single moment that reads as small, light or faked. |

**Pass rule.** Panel of ≥ 3 (evaluation agents, with founders when available). Median overall ≥ 8, and no scenario median ≤ 6.

## 8. Log schema `gait_log@v1` (contract between the mech implementation and the metric tool)

**Format.**
- JSON Lines, UTF-8, one record per line, field `rec` ∈ {`header`, `step`, `event`, `frame`}.
- Exactly one `header`, on the first line. `step` records come every fixed physics step, in order, with no gaps. `event` records are written immediately after the `step` of the same `n`. `frame` records come once per rendered frame.
- File name: `evidence/<task>/gait/<capture>.gait.jsonl`.

**Units and frame.**
- SI only (m, kg, s, N, rad).
- World frame = Unity world axes (left-handed, +y up, +x east, +z north). Positions are **absolute float64** (floating-origin offset added back).
- Quaternions are `[x, y, z, w]` (Unity order).
- Yaw ψ = atan2(fwd.x, fwd.z) of the root forward axis, wrapped to (−π, π]; the tool unwraps it. Positive yaw is clockwise seen from above (Unity convention).
- Vectors are 3-arrays.

**`header` fields (all required unless marked optional).**
- `schema`: `"gait_log@v1"`
- `capture`: scenario name; one of `walk_fr010`, `walk_fr020`, `walk_fr030`, `start`, `stop`, `estop`, `turn90_inplace`, `curve`, `stomp`, or free text.
- `commit`: git hash of the build.
- `unit_id`: string. `unit_kind`: `mech` | `kaiju`.
- `H_m`, `leg_length_m` (the l used in Fr), `com_height_stand_m`, `mass_kg`, `I_yaw_kgm2`.
- `foot_ids`: array (any count; D-004 spirit: no hard-coded 2).
- `feet`: array of objects, one per foot:
  - `id`;
  - `length_m`;
  - `width_m`;
  - `sole_points_local`: five `[x, y, z]` points in the foot frame; foot origin = sole centre, local +z = toe, local +y = sole normal.
- `fixed_dt_s`: e.g. 0.02. `physics_hz`. `render_hz_target`.
- `g_mps2`: 9.81. `mu_assumed`. `reactor_power_W`.
- `T_stride_nom_s`: from the config for the commanded Fr. `fr_cruise_nom`.
- `thresholds`: `{F_on_N, F_off_N}` (the values the implementation used for its `FootContact` event).
- `camera_rig`: string.
- `notes`: optional.

**`step` fields** (one per physics step):

| Field | Type / unit | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `n` | int | physics step index, starting at 0 |
| `t` | float64 s | simulation time = n·fixed_dt |
| `com_p`, `com_v` | m, m/s | whole-body CoM position and velocity (all articulation links) |
| `root_p`, `root_q` | m, quat | pelvis/root link pose |
| `yaw`, `yaw_rate` | rad, rad/s | root heading and its rate |
| `feet` | array, same order as header `foot_ids` | each element: `{p, q, v, contact, fn, ft, cn}` |
| └ `p`, `q`, `v` | m, quat, m/s | foot-frame origin pose and velocity |
| └ `contact` | 0/1 | the implementation's contact state (its F_on / F_off hysteresis) |
| └ `fn` | N | summed normal contact force on the foot |
| └ `ft` | N, 3-vec | summed tangential contact force |
| └ `cn` | unit 3-vec | force-weighted mean contact normal (null when not in contact) |
| `ext_f` | N | magnitude of non-ground external force on the body (0 in locomotion captures) |
| `intent` | object | `{move, target, effort, cmd_speed_mps, cmd_yaw_rate_rps}`. `move`, `target` and `effort` come from `IntentFrame` (MODULARITY §4.1); the other two are the locomotion controller's commands. |
| `p_loco_W` | W, optional | actuator power drawn for locomotion |

**`event` fields:** `{rec:"event", n, t, type, ...}` where `type` is one of:
- `contact_begin`: `{foot, contact_id, fn, foot_v}`
- `contact_end`: `{foot, contact_id}`
- `cam_impulse`: `{foot, contact_id, amplitude_m, dir, source_n}`. `source_n` is the n of the triggering `contact_begin`.
- `footstep_audio`: `{foot, contact_id, t_audio_s}`
- `intent_change`: `{move, cmd_speed_mps, cmd_yaw_rate_rps}`

**`frame` fields:** `{rec:"frame", f, t_render, n_latest, cam_p, cam_q, cam_impulse_offset_m}`. `n_latest` is the last physics step included in this frame; `cam_impulse_offset_m` is the impulse contribution actually applied to the camera this frame.

## 9. Metric specs

### `M-001@v1` Feels heavy
**Definition.** The mech's locomotion is dynamically similar to a large, heavy biped. Speed, timing, accelerations, turn rates and start/stop times stay inside physics-derived limits, and a panel judges it to feel like 10,000 t.

**Inputs.** `gait_log@v1` files for the captures `walk_fr010`, `walk_fr020`, `walk_fr030`, `start`, `stop`, `estop`, `turn90_inplace`, `curve`. Each capture has ≥ 3 strides of steady state where applicable, flat terrain, `ext_f` = 0. The panel captures are listed in §7.

**Procedure.**
1. **Smoothing.** CoM acceleration is the central difference of `com_v`, then a centred moving average of width 0.05 T_nom (0.33 s at reference) to remove solver contact noise. Yaw rate gets the same filter.
2. **Strides.** Stride boundaries are successive `contact_begin` events of the same foot. Stride-averaged speed v̄ = horizontal CoM displacement / stride time.
3. **Steady segment.** `cmd_speed` is constant, and |Δv̄| between consecutive strides < 0.02 g·T.
4. **Normalisation.** All limits are computed from header values (l, h, L_f, W_f, m, P), so variants and kaiju reuse the same tool.

### `M-002@v1` No foot sliding
**Definition.** Planted feet do not slide. For every contact c, the slip s_c (§5) is < 0.5 % of foot length.

**Inputs.** `gait_log@v1` from all M-001 locomotion captures plus `stomp`, and a 20-stride `walk_fr020` on non-flat Bay terrain (slope ≥ 5°) when the terrain exists.

**Procedure.** Segment contacts with the F_on / F_off hysteresis on `fn`. For each contact, compute s_c from the sole points (header `sole_points_local`, foot `p`, `q`) as in §5, project onto the tangent plane of n̄_c, and drop contacts with `ext_f` > 0.05 m g.

## A. Derivation code (kaiju env)
A.1 Gait table: `v = sqrt(Fr*g*l); lam = 2.3*Fr**0.3*l; T = lam/v` with l = 0.53·80 = 42.4. Human check at l = 0.93 m. Results are in §1.
A.2 Yaw inertia:
- Each segment i contributes I_i = m_i (r_i²/2 + d_i²). The trunk is an elliptic cylinder, m(a² + b²)/4. Feet are boxes, (L² + W²)/12 + d².
- Mass fractions: trunk 0.497, head-neck 0.081, upper arm 0.028, forearm 0.016, hand 0.006, thigh 0.100, leg 0.0465, foot 0.0145 [15].
- Heroic widths (×H): a 0.13, b 0.08, arm offset 0.17, hip offset 0.06, segment radii 0.015–0.04.
- Result: 4.75×10⁸ kg·m² (heroic); 2.63×10⁸ (human widths); 0.86 kg·m² for a 70 kg, 1.75 m human. Confidence: medium (the model is mine; only the segment masses are sourced).
A.3 Traction torque: mean sole distance r̄ = 5.53 m (numerical integral over 19.6 × 7.65 m). T = μ m g d + μ m g r̄ (upper bound).
A.4 Power-limited start: integrate m dv/dt = min(0.30 m g, (P − c m g v)/v) from rest to 0.9 × 9.12 m/s: 2.79 / 2.99 / 3.88 s at 6 / 10 / 15 kt.
A.5 Pendulum CoM descent at heel strike: half-angle asin(15.05/42.4) = 20.8°, v_z ≈ 3.2 m/s. Loading window 0.1 T = 0.66 s.
