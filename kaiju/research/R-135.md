## 2. Structural model (engine-free, deterministic)
A graph: nodes with mass, edges are elements.
- **Main cable**: polyline of `CableSegment` nodes at hanger points (about 85 per main span cable; side spans 343 m each; backstay to anchorage) = tension-only elements with EA and rest length, breaking force F_u, strain-to-failure 0.03 (**Assumed**). Initial shape: catenary/parabola under dead load solved by static relaxation (a deterministic iteration on node positions), so rest lengths are set from the loaded geometry (form-finding step).
- **Hangers**: tension-only vertical elements node(cable)-node(deck); slack at zero tension; breaking force F_h = 2.5 x design hanger tension (Assumed); EA from rope area.
- **Deck / stiffening truss**: chain of rigid segments (15.2 m) joined by moment-limited beam-joints; bending capacity from truss depth and chord area (**Assumed**, T-188 step 1 sources a section table); shear capacity at joints.
- **Towers**: column elements (two legs + portals) as beam-columns; yield/buckling check by Euler buckling P_cr = pi^2 EI/(K L)^2 with steel E = 200 GPa, plus plastic moment M_p at the base; saddle fixity vs slide (cable can slip on saddle at > friction limit, Assumed mu 0.2).
- **Anchorages**: fixed nodes (concrete mass), cable pull-out not modelled (Assumed).
Solver: **XPBD** (extended position-based dynamics; Macklin et al. 2016 [S4, not re-read]) with compliance alpha = 1/(EA/L0) for tension-only constraints (unilateral: active when stretched), fixed substeps (dt = 1/240 s, 20 substeps; **Assumed**), fixed iteration order, deterministic integer-seeded; broken constraints removed at force > F_u; fragments: when a deck segment loses all hangers/joints it becomes a rigid body (mass from truss; falls under gravity, impacts water with drag; ballistic) using the same solver in free flight, with no engine physics. Progressive collapse "unzipping": hanger-by-hanger cascade emerges because removing a hanger raises tension in its neighbours (load redistribution); the dynamic amplification factor of sudden member loss is 2 (static duality; GSA/DoD UFC 4-023-03 uses DIF 2 for instantaneous removal [S5, not re-read]) so a unit step check uses 2x static neighbour load. Failure criteria summary: cable F > F_u; hanger F > F_h (snap, with release energy); tower base M > M_p or P > P_cr (buckling); deck joint M/V > capacity.
Scale of the model: about 85 cable nodes x 2 cables x 2 towers + side spans; < 600 nodes, < 1,200 constraints: **trivially cheap** (about 0.1 to 0.5 ms per frame at 20 substeps, my estimate; Assumed).

## 3. What a kaiju strike must do (energy scales; R-027 for source energies)
R-027: heavy 406 mm-class shell = 356 MJ muzzle energy; melee default 18 MJ (swing KE at 30 m/s), Max preset 75 MJ, Froude-similar upper bound 460 MJ [R-027 section 1/2, local].
- **Strain energy to break a cable length**: energy density about 0.5 sigma_u epsilon_u = 16 to 32 MJ/m^3 (1.6 GPa, strain 2 to 4 %, Assumed) x A = 0.515 m^2 = **8 to 16 MJ per metre of cable** (my arithmetic). Wires fail where weakest, but a local transverse strike must stretch some metres (the free length between hangers 15 m, load-sharing) so breaking a main cable needs about 10^2 to 10^3 MJ localised, i.e. **order of the heaviest shell or an Upper-bound melee blow**; a default melee (18 MJ) or one shell will not snap a main cable. Expect cable snap only from a *sustained* sequence (cable wires break first on a ripped-up section) or from cascading load shift. (Order-of-magnitude, my arithmetic.)
- **Hanger rope**: a hanger of 0.002 m^2 area, length 50 m, energy at break roughly 0.5 sigma_u epsilon_u x A x L about 0.5 x 1.6e9 x 0.03 x 0.002 x 50 = 2.4 MJ (my arithmetic): **one default melee blow (18 MJ) can snap several hangers** (about 5 to 7 if fully efficient; Assumed 30 % transfer efficiency -> 2).
- **Tower**: 227 m steel cellular tower, base plastic moment. A lateral impact at height h with energy E: tower absorbs elastic E = 0.5 F^2 / k; with k about 1e7 N/m horizontal (**Assumed**) 18 MJ gives F about 19 MN, a base moment of 19 MN x 100 m = 1.9 GN m vs M_p (**Assumed** 5 GN m class): permanent damage plausible, collapse requires multiple heavy hits; this is the gameplay "tower break".
- **Deck**: a mech or kaiju stepping on the deck, direct crush: mass from the T-005 reference body; deck line load 150 to 250 kN/m (Assumed total) is the static baseline; a 10,000 t body standing on the deck is 98 MN over perhaps 40 m, i.e., 2.5 MN/m = 10 x the entire design line load (my arithmetic): **the deck fails by punching/shear** when a mech walks on it; the bridge cannot carry a giant. This is a key design truth to surface.
Strike energy input is `Apply(id, energyJ, cause, source, step)` already used by buildings; for bridge elements the strike resolves to *element* hits through the contact point, via a new `IStructureDamage.ApplyImpact(structureId, contactPoint, energyJ, impulse, direction)` (see T-188).
