# R-007 Why the first walk misses M-001, and the ranked path to Froude 0.20 (F-001, F-003, M-001, M-006)

**Question (orchestrator).** The first physics walk is stable and honest, but cruises at Fr 0.02 (2.86 m/s) against the registered cruise band 0.17–0.23 (`M-001.1`: "cruise command within ±0.03 of `fr_cruise_nom` (0.20 …)"). Why, is Fr 0.20 reachable with this body, and what is the minimal set of controller changes?

**Inputs.** Body: `Assets/Game/Config/Mech/mech_frame_balanced.json` (segment lengths, masses, ROM, 15 × 10 m sole). Gait relations and `M-001@v1`: R-001 §1, §2, §9. Joints, gains, power, control loop, `M-006@v1`: R-005 §2–§5, §8. Build facts as stated in the brief (treated as data): v = 2.86 m/s for all walk commands; step ≈ 0.3 l; flat-footed, no heel-off, toe-off or hip vault, knee and ankle at their limits; swing-leg yaw and pitch reaction beats stance friction (trunk yaw, foot twist, bad landings); touchdown peak 4.49 BW (limit 2.0); peak joint power 2,485 MW (limit 450); curve and 180° reversal fall; stand, start, stop, turn in place, capture step under 0.3 m·g, fall all work; μ = 0.6.

**Derivations.** All numbers below come from `research/R-007/derive.py` (kaiju env, reads the JSON): `/opt/miniconda3/envs/kaiju/bin/python research/R-007/derive.py`. l = 42.4 m (R-001), hip joint at 42.0 m, thigh 19 m, shank 19 m, ankle 4 m above the sole, heel 5.25 m behind and toe 9.75 m ahead of the ankle. One leg = 2.0e6 kg (20 % of mass).

**Headline.** The miss is a *controller* limit, not a body limit. A flat-footed gait with flexed stance knees is capped by the 20° ankle dorsiflexion at 0.34–0.37 l (14.5–15.6 m), which matches the measured 0.3 l. Heel-to-toe rollover with a 20° pre-swing knee bend reaches 33.6 m with 5° drive margin on every joint, more than the 30.1 m that Fr 0.20 needs. The impact and power overshoots are 2–5× above what inverted-pendulum walking predicts even at the measured speed, so they are also controller-made. Fr 0.20 is reachable with the registered thresholds; no data change and no threshold change is required. Two measurement clarifications are proposed.

## 1. Kinematic reachability

**Targets** (R-001: λ/l = 2.3 Fr^0.3; `M-001.2` lets the stride period range over 0.85–1.15 of Alexander's, so the step can range over 0.85–1.15 of nominal at fixed speed):

| Fr | v (m/s) | nominal step (m, /l) | step time (s) | steps/min | accepted step range (m) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.02 (measured) | 2.86 | 15.1 (0.36) | 5.23 | 11.5 | 12.8–17.3 |
| 0.17 (band floor) | 8.41 | 28.7 (0.68) | 3.41 | 17.6 | 24.4–33.0 |
| **0.20** | **9.12** | **30.1 (0.71)** | **3.30** | **18.2** | **25.6–34.6** |
| 0.23 (band ceiling) | 9.78 | 31.4 (0.74) | 3.21 | 18.7 | 26.7–36.1 |

**Reach model.** Sagittal plane, double-support instant, pelvis upright, both contacts on the ground at one pelvis height. JSON joint limits: hip flexion −20/+120, knee 0/135, ankle plantarflexion −20/+40 (negative = dorsiflexion); pelvis yaw limited by hip internal rotation (−40/+35). Step = heel-to-heel distance once the feet are flat. The script maximises step length over all poses that meet the limits (SLSQP, 25 random starts).

| Case | Max step | Binding limits (rear / front leg) |
|---|---|---|
| A. Flat feet, knees straight (knees free, optimum is straight) | **26.0 m (0.61 l)**, pelvis 39.7 m | rear hip −20 and ankle −20; front ankle +20 |
| A with 5° drive margin | 19.7 m (0.46 l) | same |
| A′. Flat feet, stance knees ≥ 20° / ≥ 30° | **15.6 / 14.5 m (0.37 / 0.34 l)** | rear ankle −20 (shank pitched over a flat foot) |
| B. + rollover (front heel strike to −25° toe-up, rear heel rise), knees straight | 32.4 m (0.76 l) | rear hip −20, ankle −20 |
| B with 5° margin, heel strike ≥ −15°, heel rise ≤ 20° | 24.7 m (0.58 l) | rear hip −15 |
| C. B + pelvic rotation (optimum 6.6°) | 33.9 m (0.80 l) | adds 13 sin ψ: +1.5 m at 6.6°, +1.8 m at 8° |
| D. C + free knees (pre-swing knee bend of the trailing leg) | 54.4 m (1.28 l) | a lunge with rear knee 40° and heel rise 40°, an upper bound only |
| **D realistic: 5° margin, heel strike ≥ −15°, heel rise ≤ 20°, pelvis ≤ 8–10°** | **33.6–34.0 m (0.80 l)** | rear hip −15 (margin edge), rear knee 20°, rear ankle −15 |

**Findings.**
- The measured 0.3 l step is what a flat-footed gait with a flexed stance knee permits (A′: 0.34–0.37 l at the ankle limit, less with any margin). This explains "knee and ankle reach their limits". Even a straight-kneed flat-footed gait cannot reach Fr 0.20 with margin (19.7 m < 25.6 m). Confidence: high (geometry from the data).
- Rollover is the enabling change. Heel strike, foot-flat, heel rise and toe-off let the leg use the 15 m foot as a lever. Together with a 20° pre-swing knee bend on the trailing leg, the step reaches 33.6 m with 5° margin on every joint, which covers the whole Fr 0.17–0.23 band. This matches the human determinants of gait: heel rise and foot rollover contribute far more than pelvic rotation [6][20]. Pelvic rotation is worth only +1.8 m at 8° (human 3–6° [6][20]).
- **ROM data: no change required.** The binding joint is hip extension (−20°), with ankle dorsiflexion (−20°) next. With the realistic case D, Fr 0.23 (step 31.4 m) still has about 2 m of reach in hand. Optional proposal for margin only: hip extension −20° → −25° (human passive range 20–30° [R-005 ref 4]). See Q-NEW-a below. Default: no change.

## 2. Dynamic feasibility

### 2.1 Swing leg (2.0e6 kg, I_hip = 1.03e9 kg·m², CoM 18.0 m below the hip)
- **Pendular half-period:** 5.35 s with a straight leg, 5.11 s with the knee at 60° (I = 8.4e8, d = 16.2 m). **Required swing time** at Fr 0.20 (T = 6.60 s): 2.64 / 2.31 / 1.85 s for duty factor β = 0.60 / 0.65 / 0.72. So the leg must swing 2.0–2.9× faster than a passive pendulum. Humans have the same ratio: the half-period scaled by 1/6.72 is 0.80 s against a human swing of about 0.4 s. That is consistent with dynamic similarity [1][14]. Swing cost rises steeply with frequency [23], so the hip pattern decides the power.
- **Swing as a single-support quintic** (the thigh starts swinging forward only at lift-off, ±20° hip excursion, I_eff 9.3e8): peak hip torque 0.78 / 0.97 / 1.42e9 N·m. Peak hip power 290 / 397 / 696 MW for one hip, at β = 0.60 / 0.65 / 0.72. Yaw moment on the stance foot peaks at 2.05 / 2.48 / 3.52e8 N·m.
- **Swing started in pre-swing** (the thigh reverses during double support, driven by ankle push-off and the hip flexors, as in human A2/H3 power bursts [15]): peak hip torque 4.6e8 N·m, peak power 116 MW, mean 15 MW at β = 0.65. These are inside the hip forceLimit of 1.2e9 and match R-005's scaled human figure of 47–97 MW. Single-support yaw moment falls to 1.07–1.35e8 N·m. **This halves torque and yaw and cuts power by 3.4×. It is the single most important dynamic change.**

### 2.2 Stance-foot friction and pitch budget (15 × 10 m sole, μ = 0.6, 1 BW, uniform pressure)
- Free-moment capacity M_max = μ W r̄ (r̄ = 4.85 m) = **2.85e8 N·m**. It is 2.69e8 while also carrying 0.2 W of fore-aft shear (elliptic limit surface). It is **1.65e8** under M-002's 1.5 safety factor (μ_use 0.4, shear 0.2 W). On a 3 m heel or toe strip it is only 1.6e8 at full μ: a foot loaded on its edge cannot hold yaw.
- Against the budget of 1.65e8: the single-support quintic swing exceeds it at every β (2.05–3.52e8). This is consistent with the observed trunk yaw and foot twist. A pre-swing-started swing fits at 1.07–1.35e8 (65–82 % of budget).
- **Pitch.** CoP lever × 1 BW gives a capacity of 5.15e8 N·m on the heel side and 9.56e8 on the toe side. Accelerating the swing thigh forward pitches the trunk back and needs CoP toward the heel. With the single-support quintic, the hip torque (7.8e8–1.4e9) exceeds the heel capacity, which means bad landings. With the pre-swing start, the 4.6e8 peak falls in double support, where both feet share it.

### 2.3 Cancelling the yaw reaction
- **Arm counter-swing** (one arm 7.0e5 kg, CoM 16.7 m below the shoulder, lever arm ±13.5 m, about twice the hip's 6.5 m). Cancelling the stride-frequency component of the legs' vertical angular momentum (peak 1.9e8 kg·m²/s) takes **±25.8° shoulder flexion** (half: ±12.5°). Shoulder ROM is −50/+160. The arm's pendulum period is 9.27 s against the 6.60 s stride, so the shoulder adds stiffness 1.12e8 N·m/rad: peak 4.9e7 N·m and about 10 MW per arm. Arms remove most of the *angular momentum* but only about 8–15 % of the *peak moment*, because the moment peak is a higher harmonic. Collins et al. measured arm swing cutting the human vertical ground-reaction moment by about 63 % [7]. Whole-body angular momentum is tightly regulated in humans [8].
- **Trunk counter-rotation (waist yaw, I_upper 3.78e8, ROM ±30°).** Absorbing all of the legs' angular momentum would need ±18° of thorax yaw (visible, but inside the ROM). After the arms cancel the fundamental, it needs ±3.3° (human thorax 3–7°). The waist torque equals the residual moment, ≤ 1.2e8 against a 5e8 forceLimit. This is the WABIAN trunk-moment compensation [18] and Kajita's resolved momentum [16].
- **Pelvic rotation** cancels nothing: the pelvis rotates with the swing leg. It helps only through step length (−1.4° of hip excursion at 30 m, about −7 % of yaw and torque).
- **Combined:** pre-swing start + arms ±25° + waist ±3° drives the ground free moment toward zero. The design target is ≤ 50 % of the 1.65e8 budget.

### 2.4 Touchdown impact
- Inverted pendulum, CoM height ≈ 40 m in double support. The vertical CoM velocity at transition is v sin α, where α is half the angle between the contact-to-CoM lines. With **point contacts under the ankles (compass)** at Fr 0.20 and 30.1 m: α = 20.6°, v_y = 3.21 m/s. With **rollover** (contacts at the rear toe and front heel, 15 m closer together): α = 10.7°, **v_y = 1.69 m/s**. This is the rolling-foot benefit [4][5].
- Peak vertical force if the redirection −v_y → +v_y is spread as a half-sine over double support (0.66 / 0.99 / 1.45 s at β = 0.60 / 0.65 / 0.72): compass 2.56 / 2.04 / 1.71 BW; **rollover 1.82 / 1.55 / 1.37 BW**. If it is done by collision alone over 0.2 s: 3.71 BW. With push-off doing half the redirection just before heel strike [2][3]: 2.35 BW at 0.2 s, 1.82 at 0.33 s, 1.54 at 0.5 s. Human walking peaks at 1.1–1.3 BW at this Froude number [15].
- **At the measured gait** (2.86 m/s, 12.7 m) the same model predicts v_y 0.45 m/s and **≤ 1.16 BW**. So about 3.3 of the 4.49 BW measured do not come from walking dynamics. Probable sources are a swing target below ground with swing-phase gains, a stance-gain switch at contact, or a foot slap. Assumed: the evidence should log v_y of the foot and of the CoM at the contact frame, plus drive force per joint (`physics_log@v1` has the fields).
- **Compliance.** Taking the full 1.69 m/s with +1 BW of extra force needs a leg stiffness of 3.4e8 N/m over 0.29 m of travel. A 15° / 20° loading-response knee bend gives 0.33 / 0.58 m of hip drop at a knee moment of 3.7 / 4.9e8 N·m at 1.5 BW, inside the 8e8 forceLimit. Use 15° at Fr 0.20 (human 15–20° [6][22]). 30° costs 7.2e8 (90 % of the limit): avoid it.

### 2.5 Mechanical power
- Mean: CoT_mech 0.05–0.10 [R-005 §3.3] × m g v gives **45–89 MW**, against the 300 MW reactor (Assumed, Q-NEW-b of R-005).
- Peak: the human whole-body joint-power peak at push-off is 3.5–4.5 W/kg; × √45.2 = 6.72 gives **235–303 MW**. Pre-swing hip (116 MW) plus ankle push-off (168–235 MW, R-005) gives about 285–350 MW. Both are under the 450 MW peak with a 1.3–1.9× margin.
- The measured 2,485 MW is 5.5× the limit at a tenth of the target Froude number. It is a controller artifact (drives fighting each other or impacts), not a requirement.

## 3. Verdict on the target

**Reachable: yes, with this body and these limits, under honest physics.** Dynamic similarity puts Fr 0.20 at the dynamic equivalent of a human walking at 1.35 m/s. Every registered threshold is a human-like dimensionless value, so they are mutually consistent at this speed:

| Threshold | Prediction at Fr 0.20 (rollover, pre-swing, β 0.62–0.66) | Consistent? |
|---|---|---|
| Duty factor 0.60–0.72 | 0.62–0.66 recommended (swing 2.2–2.5 s; at β > 0.68 hip power and yaw rise steeply, e.g. 0.72 ⇒ 1.85 s swing) | yes, but use the lower half |
| Stride-period ratio 0.85–1.15 | step 30–34 m reachable (§1) | yes |
| Peak fore–aft accel ≤ 0.30 g | push-off and braking ≈ 0.2 BW in humans [15] ⇒ ≈ 0.2 g | yes |
| Peak vertical force 1.0–2.0 BW | 1.37–1.82 BW without knee compliance, ≈ 1.2–1.5 with it | yes; compass (no rollover) at β 0.60 gives 2.56, **so the 2.0 cap effectively mandates rollover** |
| Peak power ≤ 450 MW | 285–350 MW | yes (pre-swing start required: a single-support swing alone makes 400–700 MW) |
| Mean power vs 300 MW reactor | 45–89 MW | yes |
| μ 0.6 with the M-002 1.5 margin | free moment 1.1–1.35e8 against a 1.65e8 budget | yes, tight without arms |

**No threshold looks physically wrong.** Two measurement definitions are ambiguous enough to fail an honest gait, so two clarifications are proposed (not relaxations):
- **`M-006@v1.1` (6.6):** "peak Σ P ≤ 450 MW" should read *peak of Σ positive joint mechanical power, averaged over a 0.1 s window; Σ |P| reported separately*. Derivation: the reactor supplies positive work only (R-005 §3.3, no regeneration). A single 1/60 s contact spike carries negligible energy (450 MW × 1/60 s = 7.5 MJ, 0.5 % of the 1.5 GJ buffer). The 0.1 s window is 0.015 T_stride, finer than M-001's 0.05 T smoothing.
- **`M-006@v1.1` (6.4, walking):** the peak F_n should be filtered like M-001 (0.05 T centred moving average) before it is compared with 1.0–2.0 BW. Without a filter, one solver step of contact stiffening sets the peak; the quantity of interest is the GRF that a force plate under a heavy walker would show.

**Staged plan and measurements.**
- **Stage 1 = #1 (+ #5 schedule).** Expect 5–6 m/s (Fr 0.06–0.09). Measure the per-foot `physics_log` footfall peak F_n (filtered), v_y of the CoM and the foot at contact, Σ positive P (0.1 s), knee angle at loading, step length and time per command, and M-001.1–.3.
- **Stage 2 = #2.** Expect 7–8 m/s (Fr 0.12–0.15). Additionally measure the CoP path along the sole per stance, ankle push-off torque, power and timing against contralateral heel strike, foot pitch at heel strike and toe-off, and ankle and hip joint margins to their limits (≥ 5°).
- **Stage 3 = #3.** Expect 9.1 m/s (Fr 0.20 ± 0.03). Measure swing duration, β, hip peak torque and power, the stance-foot free moment against the 1.65e8 budget, M-002 slip, and M-006.6.
- **Stage 4 = #4 + #6.** Same speed with margin. Measure trunk yaw amplitude, free-moment utilisation (target ≤ 50 %), arm power, and then rerun the Froude sweep 0.10–0.30 (R-001 calibration capture (a)).
Speeds per stage are *Assumed* estimates from §1–§2 (scaling the step and swing time). Confidence: medium.

## 5. Turning while walking and reversal (brief)
- **Why they fall (derived, consistent with the build data).** (a) In a curve the CoM needs a centripetal a = v²/R, so the CoP must sit outboard of the CoM by a/ω₀² = a h/g. At the M-001.5 lateral limit (0.834 m/s², R = 100 m at 9.12 m/s) that is **3.6 m**, close to the 5 m half-width of the sole. A placement law that keeps the straight-line ±6.5 m lateral offset runs out of CoP and the lateral DCM diverges. (b) Turning by twisting the loaded foot spends the yaw budget the swing leg already uses (§2.2). (c) A 180° reversal at speed asks for more heading change per step than hip rotation (≤ 35°) and the 5.3°/s cap at cruise (M-001.9, about 17° per 3.3 s step) allow.
- **Smallest fix.** (1) Curvature limit κ ≤ min(0.085 g / v², ψ̇_max / v), applied to the command (stride-averaged yaw rate ≤ 5.3°/s at cruise). (2) Foot placement in the rotated heading frame: p_next = ξ_end − b·ĥ(ψ_next) ± w/2·n̂(ψ_next) + (v²κ/ω₀²)·n̂_out, with heading change per step ≤ min(ψ̇_max T_step, 20°). The swing foot is pre-yawed in the air; the stance foot never twists while loaded (R-005 §5.2.7). (3) Include the turn's yaw acceleration I_z ψ̈ in the free-moment check of #4. (4) Reversal or any heading change > 45°: decelerate below 0.5 v_cruise (or stop), turn in place (already works), restart. Do not invent a new manoeuvre.
