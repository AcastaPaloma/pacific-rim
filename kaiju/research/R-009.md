# R-009 Whole-body QP design for the mech walk (T-005 round 4, step 9.1; builds on R-008 §3 option b)

Status: design note, no code run. Tags: **VERIFIED** = the Unity 6000.6.9f1 doc page was read; **ASSUMED** = not in the docs, to be probed in 9.5 (first Unity run). The installed UnityEngine assembly was not inspected here.

## Decision
**Centroidal two-stage controller.** Stage 1 is a small QP for contact forces (momentum, friction cone, CoP, torque limits). Stage 2 is a joint-acceleration resolution (swing foot, pelvis, posture in the null space of the stance contact and the stage-1 momentum), then a torque map. Reasons:
1. Stage 1 needs no mass matrix, so its answer survives any error in M. It acts directly on what round 3 lacked: the contact forces and the momentum, yaw included.
2. It is small (below) and meets the 2 ms budget with margin. A single full inverse-dynamics QP has about 52 variables (20 accelerations + 32 force generators) and costs about 3x more.
3. Joint inertia errors only enter the feed-forward torque. The PD drive of imp 3 corrects them.
4. Upgrade path: if 9.3 shows stage 1 and 2 disagree, merge them into one QP with the same pieces (dynamics rows = the equalities of stage 2).

**Building M.** `GetJointForcesForAcceleration` returns only the called body's own DOF block, so it cannot give a column of M. Use instead
`M = sum_i J_i^T diag(m_i I3, R_i I_i R_i^T) J_i` from `GetDenseJacobian` and the link masses and inertias. This is exact if the Jacobian reference point is the link COM (ASSUMED; if it is the link origin, shift by `r x`). The cost is 15 x (6x20x20) flops. The centroidal momentum matrix `A_G` is the same sum with the shift to the CoM. Then:
- Bias: `h = G + C` straight from the engine (gravity and Coriolis lists). Sign of the "counteract" list is ASSUMED to be +g(q) in `M qdd + C + g = tau + J^T f`; check with a static pose.
- Cross-check M (9.5): diag(M) block of each inbound joint against `GetJointForcesForAcceleration(1)`, and a free-swing leg acceleration after a known jointForce.
- Floating base: column order (6 base DOF first?), row order (= `ArticulationBody.index`?), and the world frame of the rows are all ASSUMED. First 9.5 probe: `J qdot` against `GetPointVelocity` for every body.
- The measured momentum needs no Jacobian: `h = sum_i [m v_i ; I w_i + m r_i x v_i]` from body velocities, COM and inertia (ASSUMED property names: `velocity`, `angularVelocity`, `worldCenterOfMass`, `inertiaTensor`).
- Mass scaling: forces and masses live in sim units (`sim_mass_unit_kg` 1e5, `reference_mass_kg` 1e7). Use the same conversion as the jointForce write at ArticulatedBody.cs:321. The QP is non-dimensional: forces / (m g), torques / (m g l), l = 42.4 m, Fr = v^2/(g l) (Fr 0.10 = 6.4 m/s).

## 2. Formulation
Plant: floating base (6) + n_j joints (about 14, 15 links, ASSUMED; count from the body build). `M qdd + h = S^T tau + J_c^T f`. Contact points: 4 sole corners per foot, 8 in all (heel and toe pairs as in the reference; the lateral corners give CoP width and yaw torque from tangential forces).
- **Stage 1 (QP, forces).** f_i = sum_j beta_ij g_ij with 4 friction-pyramid generators per corner, beta >= 0. Unilateral contact, friction cone and CoP-in-support hold by construction (beta >= 0, corners at the sole extremes). Pyramid inscribed in the cone with mu_eff = 0.9 mu / sqrt2 (mu 0.8, `body_tuning.json`); 6 generators if the 29 % loss binds.
  Cost: `|| W (sum_i [f_i ; (p_i - c) x f_i] - [m(cdd_des + g) ; kdot_des]) ||^2 + w_f ||f - f_ref||^2 + w_d ||f - f_prev||^2`.
  `cdd_des = cdd_ref + Kp(c_ref - c) + Kd(cdot_ref - cdot)`, with omega = 1.2 sqrt(g/l), critically damped. `kdot_des` is the same law on the angular momentum about the CoM, with a yaw angle term.
  Torque limits: `|tau_j| <= force_limit_nm` (ankle 1e9, knee 8e8, hip 1.2e9, waist 5e8, shoulder 3e8, elbow 1.5e8) as general rows, `tau_j = -J_cj^T f + (M qdd)_j(prev) + h_j`. Added lazily (only violated rows).
- **Stage 2 (accelerations).** Unknown qdd (20). Hard: stance contact accelerations = 0 (`J_c qdd = -Jdot qdot`; Jdot qdot from the previous tick's J difference or from the Coriolis list), `A_G qdd = hdot* - Adot qdot` (the stage-1 wrench). Soft tasks in section 3. Bounds: joint accelerations from the ROM margin (5 deg, as in the reference) and the joint velocity caps in `body_tuning.json`. Then `tau = (M qdd + h - J_c^T f*)_joints`, clamped to the limits, and written as feed-forward. The PD drives keep their target at the reference angles (imp 3: `jointForce` = tau_ff; drive `forceLimit` reduced by |tau_ff|, ASSUMED to work per tick, so tau_ff + PD never exceeds the limit).

## 4. Size, solver, budget
- **Stage 1:** 16 variables (single support, 4 corners x 4) to 32 (double support). Constraints: 16-32 simple bounds (beta >= 0), up to 8 normal-force ramp rows at transitions, up to 28 lazy torque rows (2 x n_j).
- **Stage 2:** 20 variables, <= 12 equalities (6 momentum + 6 per stance foot), up to 28 torque rows (linear in qdd through M), 28 acceleration bounds.
- **Solver:** dense dual active-set (Goldfarb-Idnani) in C#, no native libraries. H gets a Tikhonov term of 1e-6 (relative) so it stays positive definite and Cholesky works; the factor is rebuilt only when the contact set changes. Warm start: previous active set, re-validated (drop constraints that became inactive), forces re-used from the last tick for the `f_prev` term. Preallocated `double[]` buffers, no allocation per tick. Iteration cap 60; on failure keep the previous tau, clamped, and raise a counter. ADMM (OSQP style, pre-factored KKT) is the fallback only if 9.2 shows cycling on degenerate cases.
- **Budget** at 60 Hz (16.7 ms; target <= 2 ms total on M5 Pro, estimates ASSUMED until measured in 9.5): state reads and `GetDenseJacobian` / bias lists 0.3 ms; M, `A_G` 0.1 ms; stage 1 <= 0.5 ms (about 30^2 x 60 x 10 flops); stage 2 <= 0.5 ms; reference sampling and logging 0.1 ms. Expect about 1.5 ms. Report p50 and p99 per tick; p99 > 2 ms is a finding, not a failure.

## 5. Reference feed (`gait_reference_opt.json`, schema `@v1`, 7 rows Fr 0.02-0.20, 40 nodes per step)
Per node, `stance` and `swing` each give hip/knee/ankle deg and dps, `foot_pitch_deg`, `ankle_x_m`, `ankle_height_m`, `*_tau_nm`, `heel/toe_fn_n`, `heel/toe_ft_n`, `heel/toe_contact`; pelvis gives `pelvis_x_rel_m`, `pelvis_z_m`, `pelvis_pitch_deg`; the row gives `schedule` (k_ds 10, k_ff 18, k_hr 40 at Fr 0.02), `v`, `step_time_s`, `leg_length_m`. Units m, s, N, N m, degrees. Interpolation in Fr and phase as `OptimisedReference` does now.
- Missing and to add in 9.3 as an additive `@v2` field set (computed from the planar model's link data): CoM x, z in the moving frame, CoM velocity and acceleration, and sagittal angular momentum about the CoM. Today only the pelvis is given; the CoM reference must not be approximated by it (R-008 §3 item 4: partial references contradict each other).
- Forces: the sum of `fn`, `ft` over heel and toe must equal `m(cdd + g)` in the data; assert this in 9.3 as a consistency test of the data.
- **Schedule:** the phase advances at `step_time_s`. A contact point is in the QP only while the schedule says loaded (heel/toe flags, `k_ds` double-support window) and the measured contact (hysteresis `contact_on_bw` 0.03, `contact_off_bw` 0.01) does not contradict. At a detected touchdown the phase is re-anchored to node 0 and stance and swing swap. Early touchdown shortens the step; late touchdown holds the phase just before node N while the swing foot descends. Normal-force bounds of an entering or leaving corner ramp over `k_ds` to avoid a step in the force command.
- Lateral: the stance/swing foot y and the lateral CoM come from the existing imp 1/2 lateral step law (DCM), not from this file.

## 6. Test plan
**9.2 solver (engine-free C#, dotnet):** random strictly convex QPs (n 5-40, m 0-80) against scipy (SLSQP and trust-constr) and a KKT-certificate check (stationarity, complementarity, feasibility within 1e-8 relative); degenerate cases (redundant constraints, duplicate rows, zero gradient, tight H); infeasible detection; warm start gives the same optimum and fewer iterations; the 16/32-variable stage-1 shape with random wrenches; no allocation after the first call; timing p50 and p99 at the stage-1 and stage-2 sizes (Debug and Release).
**9.4 Unity controller (dotnet + EditMode):** imp 0, 1, 2 byte-identical (golden gait logs); EditMode stays 443/0/2; model tests with a fixture body (M symmetric positive definite, gravity vector matches a hand case, `A_G` momentum equals the sum of link momenta); stage 1 standing: weight shared, CoP inside the foot, yaw demand within friction; torque never above `force_limit_nm` over a replayed reference; friction ratio <= 1; schedule re-anchor unit tests (early, late, missed touchdown); no allocation per tick. 9.5 probes: the section 1 layout and sign checks; compare commanded and realised contact forces and joint torques; time per tick.

## 7. 9.3 offline prototype (`Tools/GaitOpt/`, Python)
- Plant: the planar model of `stride_opt.py` (9 DOF: hip x, z, trunk lean, 3 per leg) with its M, h; point contacts at heel and toe, stiff spring-damper with regularised Coulomb friction (mu 0.8), sub-step 1/600 s, the controller at 60 Hz with zero-order hold and a one-tick delay. Controller: the same stage 1 (2D: 4 points-pairs, 2 generators each = 8 variables) and stage 2 on the planar M, using the same GI algorithm ported to numpy (or scipy as a check).
- Sanity first: replay the reference as joint PD only (the imp 2 analogue). It should show the round-3 lag; if it does not, the plant is not representative.
- **Go/no-go (all of A-C must pass, D is informational):**
  - A. Fr 0.10 unpushed, 20 steps from an initial state off by 2 % speed and 1 deg: no fall; mean speed within 5 % of v; CoM z RMS error <= 1 % l; pelvis pitch RMS <= 3 deg; foot placement RMS <= 5 % step length; 0 saturated-torque ticks beyond 1 % of ticks; friction ratio <= 1; solver success 100 %.
  - B. Push: forward and backward impulse at mid-stance of dv = 0.15 v: back to the periodic gait (speed within 5 %) within 4 steps, no fall.
  - C. Robustness at A: controller model mass and inertia off by +/-10 %; mu 0.6 in the plant; torque limit 80 %; two-tick delay.
  - D. dv = 0.30 v push, and Fr 0.15 and 0.20 runs.
  - Yaw (not in the planar model): in 9.3 run stage 1 alone in 3D on the 8-corner foot at the reference demand (`yaw_check.py`, waist 0.26 of limit) and report the friction margin.
  - No-go (A or B or C fails after one tuning round of weights): stop, report to the founders before any Unity work (options: raise to the full-ID single QP, or the gate decision Q-074 (a)).

## 8. Risks
1. Jacobian layout, frames and the sign of the gravity list are ASSUMED. Mitigation: first probes in 9.5, with checks against body velocities and a static pose.
2. PhysX contact is not rigid Coulomb: the realised force differs from the command (patch friction, torsion from anchors, R-008 §3 option b). Mitigation: friction margin 0.9, read back realised forces, yaw weight high.
3. One-tick latency and stiff, light distal links at the 1e5 mass unit may destabilise torque control. Mitigation: delay in the prototype; keep the PD drive on the reference.
4. Feed-forward plus PD can exceed the limit if `SetDriveForceLimit` does not act per tick (ASSUMED).
5. Reference data lack CoM and angular momentum (added in 9.3) and lateral motion (LIPM stays in use).
6. Reference bound constraints at 5 deg ROM margins: a small tracking error reaches the PhysX stops, whose force is not in the model. Mitigation: ROM bounds in stage 2.
7. Touchdown impact and double-support switching can make stage 1 infeasible with torque rows. Mitigation: lazy rows, ramped bounds, fall back to clamped previous torque and a counter.
8. Time per tick above 2 ms. Mitigation: fixed small sizes, no allocation, only active contacts.
9. The planar prototype passes but Unity fails (3D, yaw, contact model). Mitigation: the stop rule of 9.5 (three cycles) and the 3D stage-1 check above.
