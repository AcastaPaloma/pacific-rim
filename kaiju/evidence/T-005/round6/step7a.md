## Offline go/no-go (`scripts/foot_sim.py`, `scripts/contact_test.py`, `step7_contact_test.txt`, `step7_walk_sim.txt`; conda kaiju)
Planar model, mech_frame_balanced numbers: point-mass body on two massless legs (leg force N u + tau/l n, i.e. the ankle strategy), 15 m x 3e5 kg foot with 9 compliant sole points (spring-damper, stick-slip friction), ankle limit 1e9 N m, capture-point foothold, explicit double support, force split by weighted least squares.
* Contact test (single stance foot landed at sole pitch -12 .. +20 deg, CoP command mid-sole, pitch gain 0 .. 1e9 N m/rad): the foot is level in 0.20-0.24 s in every case, peak ankle torque 0.22-0.88 of the limit, with or without a pitch task. So heel-only / toe-only contact does not persist by itself when the load line crosses the sole and the ankle is free to move: a toe-first landing must be held by something the model lacks (the stiff stance ankle PD to the reference ankle angle, the heel pinned by the QP's stance-point task, the CoM load line outside the sole).
* Walking-level model: 2-3 steps for every landing pitch and every CoP-bound rule (base / measured bounds / flat task), identical failure (the capture-point stepping with step time 6.27 s and 18 m steps is not self-sustaining in this simplification). It does not discriminate the designs, so it is no evidence for or against 7a.
* Go/no-go: GO for the sole-level task (cheap, cannot hurt the contact physics), NO-GO as a stand-alone cure: the model says landing orientation is not what keeps the foot rolled.

1. The baseline lands toe first (+17 deg heel up at first contact, +23 deg 0.3 s later): the round-5 diagnosis is confirmed. With the foot-pitch row on the commanded level pitch (no drive) the sole is level at first contact (-2 deg) and flat 0.3 s later: item 1 works as a landing orientation task. The ankle drive override (`qp_foot_drive` 1) breaks it (+16 deg): the swing-leg ankle PD (scaled 0.05) and the stance ankle PD (stance_hz x inertia, qp_pd_scale 0.25) fight the QP; that drive is dropped from the next iterations.
2. Ankle authority: the swing ankle never saturates (0-2 %); the stance ankle sits at >= 0.97 of its limit 6-13 % of the time (48 % with the drive), always while the stance foot is toe-only with the CoP at the toe 7-9 m ahead of the ankle. Research finding (foot length vs ankle torque): the ankle holds a CoP offset of at most tau_lim / F_z = 1e9 / (9.81e7 F_z/W) = 10.2 m / (F_z/W) from the ankle line (toe lever 9.75 m, heel 5.25 m): at 1.0 W the toe is reachable with 4 % margin, at 1.2 W only 8.5 m, so with the dynamic loads 1.1-1.8 W the forward 1-3 m of the sole cannot carry the CoP. A 15 m foot with a 1e9 N m ankle (body_tuning not edited) gives no toe-off authority; a toe lever under 7 m or tau_lim over 1.6e9 would be needed for 1.6 W.
3. Time series (`plan97.py`, `look97.py` on Rfoot s0, 7-16 s): the stance foot rolls onto its toe (contact phase 3, pitch 0 -> 25 deg, ankle at 0.96 limit) from 3 s after its touchdown on, while the swing foot is still 4-5 m behind its target (swing heel error 6.5 m at tau 4.7 s, 2-3 m at 8.8-10 s) and barely leaves the ground (heel height 0.4-0.9 m against 3.5 m commanded), IK failures from tau 5.8 s. The DCM is 15-17 m ahead of the stance heel (toe at 15 m) when the step ends at 7.2 s. The CoP target xcd is pinned to the heel for the first 3 s (the DCM is below the reference DCM, the planner asks for acceleration) and to the toe afterwards. The foot rolling is a consequence of the CoM leaving the sole before the swing foot arrives, not of the landing orientation.
## Conclusion / next
Landing orientation fixed (+17 -> -2 deg) without a survival gain (best qp_foot variant 21.9 s < 24.2 s control). The binding limit is the swing leg: the foot lags and drags (heel height 0.4-0.9 m against 3.5 m), the capture point leaves the toe before touchdown, and the stance ankle saturates at the toe. 7b adds the measured-contact constraints (per-point shear, CoP inside the measured contact interval) with the drive off, so that the stance foot does not roll to the toe.
